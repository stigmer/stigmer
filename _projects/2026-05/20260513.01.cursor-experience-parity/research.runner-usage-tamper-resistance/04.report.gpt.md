# Tamper-Resistant Usage Reporting from Untrusted Runners

## Executive judgement

The clearest pattern across real systems is that platforms do **not** make untrusted client telemetry the final billing authority when the platform itself is paying an upstream cost. They either meter at a provider-controlled chokepoint, move execution into provider-controlled infrastructure, or accept self-reported usage only when there is another trust anchor such as cloud IAM identity, hardware-backed attestation, or a platform-signed receipt. Twilio’s usage records are generated from Twilio account activity; Cloudflare and Fastly bill from platform-side request and usage metrics; Vercel AI Gateway exposes generation-level token and cost lookup because the gateway is on the request path; and NVIDIA GeForce NOW enforces playtime limits and resets centrally in its own service. citeturn25view0turn15view0turn15view3turn34view0turn24view0turn24view2

That means the uncomfortable but important answer for Stigmer is this: **an open-source runner executing on a user-controlled machine cannot, by itself, produce billing-authoritative Cursor usage data**. A modified runner can always lie about `inputTokens`, `outputTokens`, or “zero out” events unless a trusted third party signs the measurement or the measurement is independently observable elsewhere. Cryptographic commitments alone do not solve that problem; they can make a lie immutable, but not true. The standards and platforms that *do* solve this use attested claims or signed records from a trusted authority, such as IETF RATS/EAT, Apple’s signed App Store transaction objects, AWS Marketplace `RegisterUsage` signatures, Apple App Attest, and Google Play Integrity. citeturn28search0turn28search2turn19search12turn19search8turn37view1turn38search1turn25view7turn26view1

For your immediate production need, the right move is therefore **honest estimated usage now, reconciliation soon, stronger trust later**. Show runner-reported Cursor usage immediately, mark it as estimated or unverified, reconcile it against Cursor’s Admin API on an hourly or daily basis, and reserve final billing authority for sources you control or can verify independently. Your existing trust ladder of `BILLING_AUTHORITY`, `SERVER_OBSERVED`, and `DISPLAY_ONLY` is exactly the right abstraction for that staged model. Cursor’s published Admin API exposes token usage and `chargedCents`, but its documented usage-event schema does not expose `runId` or `agentId`, and it recommends polling at most once per hour because the data is aggregated hourly. That makes reconciliation possible, but not perfectly deterministic per run from the public API alone. citeturn4view1

## What industry precedent actually says

The dominant precedent is **provider-side metering**. Twilio’s Usage Records API describes usage as actions made by *your Twilio account* over a period, which is a server-side record of Twilio-delivered activity. Cloudflare’s billing docs walk through a request lifecycle on Cloudflare’s edge and map billable dimensions such as Worker requests and CPU time to that path; its billing docs also explicitly say usage notifications are informational and that the monthly invoice is the most reliable source for billing. Fastly likewise describes plan-usage metrics as the basis for billing and notes that those metrics are updated daily rather than in real time. citeturn25view0turn15view0turn15view2turn15view3

Cloud gaming follows the same logic. NVIDIA’s GeForce NOW does not trust the client to tell it how long a session lasted; instead it enforces membership session constraints and, as of the current FAQ, gives Performance and Ultimate members 100 hours of monthly playtime with up to 15 hours rolled over, resets centrally, and allows the purchase of additional hours after the cap is reached. That is server-enforced metering and settlement, not client-declared playtime. citeturn24view0turn24view1turn24view2

There are also important precedents for **constrained self-reporting** in environments that are not fully provider-controlled. AWS Marketplace’s metering APIs are especially relevant because they are designed for software that runs outside the seller’s own infrastructure. For AMI and container products, the customer’s workload reports usage to AWS Marketplace, but AWS constrains the reporter by requiring EC2 instance roles, ECS task roles, or IRSA identities rather than long-term keys; `MeterUsage` is idempotent on a once-per-hour basis for many modes and recorded values cannot be modified later; and `RegisterUsage` provides entitlement verification and returns a signed token, with `Nonce` available to scope a registration to a specific running instance and protect against replay. AWS’s SaaS metering model is more trust-based—AWS bills customers from the records the seller submits—but AWS explicitly recommends hourly reporting and CloudTrail-backed auditability. citeturn37view0turn37view1turn37view2turn37view3turn38search1

Fraud-heavy markets show the other common pattern: **trust the event stream operationally, but finalise later with filters, deductions, and penalties**. Google states that invalid traffic includes clicks or impressions that artificially inflate costs or earnings, uses over 200 sophisticated filters and manual review to detect bad traffic, and may withhold publisher payments or credit advertisers back after detection. AdMob explicitly distinguishes estimated earnings from finalised earnings, then applies deductions for invalid activity or advertiser payment failures. AppsFlyer’s Protect360 uses real-time fraud blocking plus post-attribution detection for up to seven days after install. In other words, where perfect prevention is hard, mature platforms do not pretend the first signal is final—they **settle later**. citeturn25view4turn25view5turn42view0turn42view1turn25view6

Finally, when a platform does want to trust something coming from an edge device, it usually adds **a trusted attester**. Google Play Integrity lets a backend check that a request came from an unmodified app installed by Google Play on a genuine device. Apple App Attest lets an app generate a cryptographic key on-device and validate app integrity with the server before the server grants access. Apple’s App Store Server API returns transaction and renewal information signed with JWS. Those examples matter because they show the form a credible Cursor receipt system would likely take: not “please trust this local callback”, but “verify this signed claim from a trusted authority”. citeturn25view7turn26view1turn17search3turn19search12turn19search8

## What this means for Cursor and Stigmer

Cursor’s public documentation confirms three facts that matter here. First, the TypeScript SDK can run **locally**, in **Cursor-hosted cloud**, or in a **self-hosted pool**, and for local mode it runs “inline in your Node process”; spend shows up in the team usage dashboard under the SDK tag. Second, Cursor’s team Admin API does expose granular usage events including token buckets and `chargedCents`. Third, the documented usage-event schema is keyed around fields such as `timestamp`, `userEmail`, `model`, `kind`, `isHeadless`, and `tokenUsage`, but not `runId` or `agentId`. Public Cursor docs also expose a Cloud Agents API and webhooks surface, but in the retrieved public material I did **not** find documentation showing per-run signed usage receipts or per-run token-usage webhooks you could verify independently. citeturn4view0turn4view1turn30search1turn30search9

That makes your present architecture split very cleanly:

Your **native harness** is already in the industry-preferred shape because Stigmer owns the metering chokepoint. Keep treating that data as `BILLING_AUTHORITY`. citeturn15view0turn25view0turn34view0

Your **Cursor harness**, by contrast, is in the classic “untrusted client with partly observable back-office settlement” shape. The runner’s `onDelta` usage callback is useful for UX and provisional accounting, but not trustworthy on its own because the code is open source and can be modified. Cursor’s Admin API can support later settlement, but because it is hourly and lacks the correlation keys you want in the documented schema, it is best treated as a **reconciliation feed**, not a real-time oracle for exact per-execution attribution. citeturn4view1

This is also why a **commitment scheme alone is not enough**. IETF RATS and EAT are about conveying attested claims from a trusted environment to a relying party. A bare client-side hash commitment would provide neither an attester nor a trusted issuer; it would only lock in whatever value the runner chose to report, truthful or not. Commitments become useful only when combined with an independent later check such as a Cursor-signed receipt or a trustworthy server-side event. citeturn28search0turn28search2

For TEEs, the answer is nuanced. In **Stigmer-managed cloud sandboxes**, confidential runtimes are plausible for a future hardening step. Nitro Enclaves can produce signed attestation documents, and Azure/GCP also provide attestation services for TEEs and confidential VMs. But Nitro Enclaves are highly constrained, with no external networking and only local channels to the parent instance, which makes them a poor fit for directly hosting a full networked SDK agent loop. In **local OSS mode on user machines**, there is no similarly portable, cross-platform trust anchor comparable to Play Integrity or App Attest for an arbitrary open-source CLI, so TEEs are not a practical near-term answer there. citeturn18search0turn18search3turn18search7turn27search0turn27search1turn25view7turn26view1

## Recommended phased approach

### Now

Ship the UX fix immediately by recording Cursor `onDelta` usage in the runner and sending it to the server as **estimated, runner-reported usage**. Surface it in the product with an explicit label such as **Estimated**, **Unverified**, or **Runner-reported**, and map it to your existing `DISPLAY_ONLY` trust level. Do not call it “bill settled” or “provider verified”. This matches what mature ecosystems do when they show a useful early number but finalise later: AdMob’s estimated-versus-finalised earnings model and Cloudflare’s “usage notifications are informational; the invoice is authoritative” model are the right UX analogies. citeturn42view0turn15view2

At the same time, start a reconciliation job against Cursor’s Admin API. Cursor documents that usage events are aggregated hourly and recommends polling at most once per hour; it also says that `chargedCents` is the field to sum to reconcile event-level costs with `/teams/spend`. For each Stigmer execution, attempt matching on the strongest available fields: user or service-account identity, model, `maxMode`, `isHeadless`, time window, and token-shape similarity. Store a confidence score and update the UI from **Estimated** to **Settled** where the match is strong enough. Where it is not, keep the execution at estimated status and settle only at the user/day or account/day level. citeturn4view1

For credits, use a **provisional hold** model rather than an irreversible debit. Payment networks routinely separate authorisation from final capture when the final total is not yet known, and Google’s ads business similarly revises estimated earnings into finalised earnings after fraud and payment checks. In Stigmer terms, place a tentative credit reservation from runner-reported usage, but only finalise the debit when either native-harness telemetry or a sufficiently confident Cursor reconciliation arrives. citeturn20search0turn20search1turn42view0turn42view1

### Soon

Reduce ambiguity before you try to reduce adversarial tampering. The best operational improvement here is to make Cursor events easier to attribute. Cursor’s SDK accepts user API keys and service account API keys. If you can issue **dedicated Cursor service accounts or API keys per Stigmer workspace, environment, or even per active execution lane**, you materially improve reconciliation because the Admin API is keyed to user/team identity and not to your run IDs. Even if this does not fully solve concurrency, it narrows the candidate set dramatically. citeturn4view0turn4view1

Add **fraud and anomaly detection** as a second line of defence, not as the billing source of truth. Borrow from AWS Cost Anomaly Detection and ad-fraud systems: track residuals between runner-reported usage and later-settled Cursor usage; watch for persistent under-reporting by user, workspace, model, or runner build; detect sharp behavioural shifts; and alert on workloads whose prompt size, response size, duration, or tool activity are inconsistent with claimed token counts. AWS documents machine-learning spend anomaly detection with seasonality-aware baselines, while Google and AppsFlyer document layered filters, behavioural analysis, and post-event review. citeturn25view3turn25view5turn25view6

Use **calibration or canary turns** only as a detector. In cloud sandboxes that Stigmer controls, you can inject a tiny server-authored operation with a known approximate token profile and flag runs that systematically misreport it. The right mental model is the security honeytoken: any trip is high-signal precisely because legitimate users should not be manipulating it. But because your runner is open source, do not assume such checks will survive scrutiny in local OSS mode; treat them as one feature in a fraud score, not a hard guarantee. citeturn39search12turn39search8turn39search3

Also add **consequences**. Google may suspend or disable accounts and withhold or claw back payments when invalid activity is detected; AppsFlyer blocks fraudulent attribution in real time and later reclassifies invalid installs. Stigmer should have an equivalent policy for Cursor harnesses: downgrade trust, stop final billing from that runner, require cloud mode, or freeze the account for review when reconciliation gaps become material or systematic. citeturn25view4turn25view5turn25view6turn42view1

### Later

Ask Cursor for the missing primitive directly: a **provider-signed per-run receipt**. There is strong precedent for this shape. Apple signs transaction and renewal records as JWS; AWS Marketplace `RegisterUsage` returns a signature and supports nonce-style replay protection; mobile attestation products bind claims to a server challenge and let the backend verify them. A good Cursor feature request would therefore be: a backend-issued JWS or JWT-style receipt, delivered both via SDK completion and optional webhook, that includes `cursor_run_id`, `agent_id`, a Stigmer-supplied `nonce` or `external_run_id`, `user/service_account`, `model`, `isHeadless`, `maxMode`, full token buckets, `chargedCents`, currency, timestamp bounds, receipt ID, and key ID, with a published JWKS for verification. The receipt must be generated by Cursor’s backend, not by the runner process. citeturn19search12turn19search8turn37view1turn38search1turn25view7turn26view1

In parallel, consider changing the execution model for high-trust or high-value workloads. Anthropic’s Managed Agents and Cursor’s Cloud Agents both illustrate the industry move from local orchestration to provider-managed runtime when strong observability and control are needed. If Stigmer can route some Cursor work through **Cursor Cloud Agents** or another managed server-side runtime, you improve auditability and reduce the attack surface introduced by arbitrary local processes. Cursor’s docs say Cloud Agents run in isolated cloud environments, support API/webhook surfaces, and can also be run via self-hosted pools, which may fit a future Stigmer-managed deployment model better than local SDK execution. citeturn35search1turn35search4turn5search1turn4view0turn30search1turn30search9

For Stigmer-operated Daytona sandboxes, the longer-horizon hardening path is **confidential VMs or attested sidecars**, not generic enclaves bolted onto local laptops. TEEs can prove workload identity to your server, but the fit is best when **you launch and own the runtime**. If you go down this path, prefer a design that attests the whole meter-carrying workload or a dedicated sidecar in a confidential VM, rather than trying to run only a tiny enclave next to an already-untrusted host process. citeturn18search0turn18search3turn18search7turn27search0turn27search1

- **Soon:** improve attribution with dedicated service accounts or execution lanes, add anomaly scoring and policy consequences, and use canary checks only for fraud detection. citeturn4view0turn25view3turn25view5turn25view6turn39search12
- **Later:** push for Cursor-signed receipts or move the highest-trust paths into managed or attested cloud execution. Until one of those exists, keep local OSS Cursor runs out of the `BILLING_AUTHORITY` bucket. citeturn19search12turn37view1turn35search1turn5search1

## Executive conclusion

For Stigmer’s Cursor harness, **runner-reported token usage is useful for UX and provisional accounting, but it is not billing-authoritative**. The core reason is not Cursor-specific; it is a general security property: when measurement happens inside user-controlled open-source code, the user can modify the code, suppress events, alter token counts, replay old events, or emit fabricated events. No local-only hash, HMAC, commitment, or “signed by the runner” scheme fixes that unless the signing key is protected by a trusted authority or trusted hardware.

The industry pattern is consistent: billing-grade metering is done at a **trusted chokepoint**, by a **provider-controlled backend**, through a **signed third-party receipt**, or inside a **remotely attested execution environment**. Twilio, Cloudflare, Fastly, Vercel AI Gateway, NVIDIA GeForce NOW, AWS Marketplace, Apple App Store, Google Play Integrity, and ad-fraud platforms all reflect variants of this pattern. Twilio usage records are generated from Twilio account activity; Cloudflare says its billable usage dashboard reads from the same system that generates invoices; Vercel AI Gateway exposes generation-level usage only because the Gateway is on the request path; NVIDIA centrally enforces monthly playtime limits; AWS Marketplace accepts customer-environment metering only with cloud identity and idempotency constraints; Apple signs App Store transactions as JWS; and Google/AppsFlyer rely on layered real-time plus post-hoc fraud detection where client-side fraud cannot be eliminated. 5. **Ask Cursor for provider-signed usage receipts or usage webhooks containing external run IDs.**

| **Require Stigmer cloud mode for paid Cursor billing**         |                                                      High |                                    Medium |                       Medium | Strongest business-control fallback: local OSS Cursor runs are display-only; billable Cursor runs must use Stigmer-controlled runtime.                                                                                                                             | Product/UX friction for local OSS users.                                                                    |

# 2. Cursor-specific findings

## 2.1 Cursor Admin API is useful but not sufficient for per-run billing

Cursor’s public Admin API includes a `/teams/filtered-usage-events` endpoint for granular usage events. The documented response includes:

```ts
usageEvents: {
  timestamp: string;
  model: string;
  kind: string;
  maxMode: boolean;
  requestsCosts: number;
  isTokenBasedCall: boolean;
  tokenUsage?: {
    inputTokens: number;
    outputTokens: number;
    cacheWriteTokens: number;
    cacheReadTokens: number;
    totalCents: number;
  };
  isFreeBugbot: boolean;
  userEmail: string;
}[]
```

That is very relevant to Stigmer: it gives provider-side token and cost data, but the documented schema does **not** include your needed `runId`, `agentId`, `externalId`, or idempotency key. ([Cursor Documentation][3])

So the Admin API should be treated as a **settlement/reconciliation feed**, not a perfect real-time per-execution meter.

Recommended matching fields:

| Signal                            |   Strength | Notes                                                                    |
| --------------------------------- | ---------: | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Cursor user/service account email |       High | Use dedicated identities to reduce collisions.                           |
| Time window                       |     Medium | Use execution start/end plus lag buffer.                                 |
| Model                             |     Medium | Must normalize Cursor model names.                                       |
| `maxMode`                         |     Medium | Useful discriminator.                                                    |
| Token shape                       |     Medium | Compare input/output/cache buckets from runner report against Admin API. |
| Total cents                       |     Medium | Use only after price normalization.                                      |
| Latency/duration                  | Low–Medium | Correlates weakly with output tokens and reasoning depth.                |
| Prompt/output byte length         | Low–Medium | Better as anomaly feature than exact matcher.                            |
| Sidecar proxy calls               |        Low | Useful for session boundaries, not usage.                                |

## 2.2 Cursor webhooks are signed, but currently not usage receipts

Cursor’s documented background-agent webhooks include HMAC-SHA256 verification headers such as `X-Webhook-Signature`, `X-Webhook-ID`, and `X-Webhook-Event`, and currently support `statusChange` events for `ERROR` or `FINISHED` states. ([Cursor Documentation][5])

That is important because it shows Cursor already has the product pattern for authenticated server-to-server callbacks. The missing feature is not “webhook signing” generally; it is **usage-bearing webhook payloads** with external correlation IDs.

A strong Cursor feature request should ask for:

```json
{
  "event": "agentUsageFinalized",
  "cursorRunId": "cur_run_...",
  "externalRunId": "stigmer_run_...",
  "agentId": "agent_...",
  "teamId": "...",
  "userEmail": "...",
  "apiKeyName": "...",
  "model": "claude-4-sonnet",
  "maxMode": false,
  "isHeadless": true,
  "startedAt": "...",
  "endedAt": "...",
  "usage": {
    "inputTokens": 10043,
    "outputTokens": 49,
    "cacheReadTokens": 2048,
    "cacheWriteTokens": 0,
    "totalCents": 12.34
  },
  "receiptId": "cur_receipt_...",
  "idempotencyKey": "..."
}
```

Signed either as:

1. HMAC webhook over raw body, like Cursor already documents for webhooks; or
2. JWS/JWT with Cursor-published JWKS, like Apple App Store Server API transactions. Apple’s `JWSTransaction` is explicitly transaction information signed by the App Store in JWS compact serialization, and Apple documents verifying the signature from the header/cert chain. ([Apple Developer][4])

## 2.3 Cursor API-key identity can help reconciliation

Cursor’s background-agent API key info endpoint returns metadata such as `apiKeyName`, `createdAt`, and `userEmail`. ([Cursor Documentation][6]) If Stigmer can create or assign dedicated Cursor API keys per workspace, user, environment, or run lane, this becomes a practical reconciliation lever.

Best near-term identity strategy:

| Granularity                         |                           Benefit |                         Operational cost |
| ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------: | ---------------------------------------: |
| One Cursor team key for all Stigmer |                  Poor attribution |                                      Low |
| One key per Stigmer workspace       | Good account-level reconciliation |                                   Medium |
| One key per workspace + environment |                            Better |                                   Medium |
| One key per active execution lane   |             Strong fuzzy matching |                                   Higher |
| One key per run                     |                  Best attribution | Possibly too much key churn / API limits |

I would start with **workspace + execution lane**, not per-run, unless Cursor key automation is cheap and rate limits permit it.

# 3. What real companies actually do

## 3.1 Twilio: provider-side records, not SDK-reported billing

Twilio’s UsageRecords API retrieves actions made by a Twilio account over a period and usage category. It exposes usage, count, price, units, and date ranges. Twilio even recommends subaccounts per end-user as a way to build recurring usage-based billing on top of Twilio. ([Twilio][1])

Pattern: **Stigmer should mimic subaccount-style attribution where possible.**

For Cursor, the analog is dedicated Cursor identities / service accounts / API keys per Stigmer workspace or execution lane. You cannot create Cursor “subaccounts” in the same sense, but you can approximate the attribution boundary.

## 3.2 Cloudflare/Fastly/CDNs: invoice comes from platform metering

Cloudflare says its billable usage dashboard reads from the same data source that generates the monthly invoice, and that completed-period usage cost should match invoice overage charges. It also says usage notifications are informational and the monthly invoice is the most reliable billing source. ([Cloudflare Docs][9])

Fastly’s Plan usage page shows usage metrics that billing is based on, but explicitly notes usage-based metrics are not real time and are updated daily. ([Fastly][10])

Pattern: **show early numbers, but settle from a trusted billing data source.**

For Stigmer, runner usage can power live UX, while Cursor Admin API powers settlement.

## 3.3 Vercel AI Gateway: usage is trustworthy because gateway is on path

Vercel AI Gateway exposes a `/generation?id=...` lookup that returns generation-level cost, usage, model, provider, latency, prompt tokens, completion tokens, reasoning tokens, and cached tokens. Generation IDs are included in chat completion responses and provider metadata. ([Vercel][2])

Pattern: **the ideal Cursor feature is a generation/run lookup API with usage and external correlation.**

This is the closest precedent to what Stigmer needs.

## 3.4 NVIDIA GeForce NOW: centrally enforced session metering

GeForce NOW paid memberships have centrally enforced monthly playtime limits, rollover rules, account-portal visibility, and additional-hour purchases when users exhaust playtime. ([NVIDIA][11])

Pattern: **cloud gaming does not trust the client to declare GPU-hours.**

The session runs in NVIDIA’s cloud; the platform can meter start/stop and enforce caps. For Stigmer, Daytona cloud mode is much closer to this model than local OSS mode.

## 3.5 AWS Marketplace: customer-environment metering with cloud identity constraints

AWS Marketplace `RegisterUsage` is relevant because marketplace software runs in the buyer’s cloud account, but metering still goes through AWS. `RegisterUsage` performs entitlement and metering, and its `Nonce` can scope registration to a specific running software instance and guard against replay. ([AWS Documentation][12])

Pattern: **self-reporting becomes more trustworthy when bound to cloud identity and idempotent provider control-plane APIs.**

For Stigmer, an analogous design would require the runner or sidecar to report usage through a Stigmer service using short-lived credentials minted only to known cloud sandboxes, not static local secrets.

## 3.6 Apple / Google mobile integrity: trusted third-party attestation

Apple App Attest explicitly says you cannot rely on app logic to check itself because a compromised app can falsify results. Instead, it creates a hardware-backed cryptographic key certified by Apple servers; servers issue challenges and verify attestations/assertions. ([Apple Developer][13])

Google Play Integrity returns integrity verdicts about whether traffic comes from a genuine app binary, installed by Google Play, on a genuine Android device; it identifies tampered app versions, untrustworthy devices, and emulated environments. ([Google Help][14])

Pattern: **client trust requires a platform trust anchor.**

There is no equivalent universal trust anchor for arbitrary Node.js open-source runners on arbitrary developer machines.

## 3.7 Mining pools: verify work, don’t trust declared work

Mining pools use “shares” as low-difficulty proof-of-work. A share demonstrates that a miner actually performed hashing work; pools accept, reject, or mark shares stale. Payouts are based on accepted shares, not on a miner’s claimed hashrate. ([Kryptex Pool][15])

Pattern: **make the client submit verifiable evidence of work, not a self-declared total.**

For LLM tokens, the equivalent would be provider-signed usage receipts or server-observed provider responses. A runner saying “I used 10,043 input tokens” is not a share; it is a claim without proof.

## 3.8 Mobile ad networks: layered fraud detection and post-hoc correction

AppsFlyer Protect360 uses real-time fraud blocking plus post-attribution detection, machine learning, behavioral analysis, and corrections after attribution. Its docs explicitly describe post-attribution fraud up to days later and invoice credits/adjustments. ([AppsFlyer Support][16])

Google Ads/AdMob similarly detects invalid clicks/impressions and credits/adjusts for invalid traffic; Google states invalid traffic is detected by monitoring systems and advertisers are not charged for invalid clicks/impressions. ([Google Help][17])

Pattern: **where prevention is impossible, mature systems separate provisional accounting from final settlement and fraud adjustment.**

That is the right pattern for Stigmer Cursor usage.

# 4. Cryptographic approaches: what works and what does not

## 4.1 Signed usage receipts

This is the best long-term solution.

A valid receipt must be signed by one of:

1. Cursor backend;
2. Stigmer-controlled server-side proxy/gateway;
3. Trusted hardware/attestation root that protects the measurement code and key.

A receipt signed by the open-source runner is not meaningful if the signing key can be extracted or the code can be modified.

Recommended receipt fields:

| Field                                                                      | Purpose                   |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| `iss = cursor.com`                                                         | Trusted issuer            |
| `aud = stigmer.ai`                                                         | Prevents misuse elsewhere |
| `receipt_id`                                                               | Deduplication             |
| `external_run_id`                                                          | Stigmer correlation       |
| `cursor_run_id`                                                            | Cursor correlation        |
| `team_id`, `user_email`, `api_key_id/name`                                 | Attribution               |
| `model`, `maxMode`, `isHeadless`                                           | Price/matching dimensions |
| `started_at`, `ended_at`, `signed_at`                                      | Timing                    |
| `input_tokens`, `output_tokens`, `cache_read_tokens`, `cache_write_tokens` | Billing units             |
| `total_cents`, `currency`                                                  | Provider-calculated cost  |
| `nonce`                                                                    | Replay protection         |
| `schema_version`                                                           | Migration                 |
| `kid`                                                                      | Key rotation              |

Use JWS/JWT with published JWKS, or HMAC webhooks if Cursor only supports per-integration secrets. Apple’s App Store Server API JWS transactions are the closest production precedent. ([Apple Developer][4])

## 4.2 TEEs and remote attestation

Remote attestation can work, but only where Stigmer controls the launch environment.

IETF RATS defines an Attester that produces Evidence, a Verifier that appraises Evidence, and a Relying Party that uses Attestation Results to decide whether to trust the Attester. ([IETF][8])

AWS Nitro Enclaves can generate signed attestation documents containing PCR measurements, nonce, and other data. Nitro Enclaves are isolated VMs with no external network connectivity and no persistent storage; even root on the parent instance cannot access enclave memory. ([AWS Documentation][18])

That makes Nitro strong but awkward for a Cursor SDK runner, because the Cursor SDK needs network access. A more practical design is:

```text
Parent VM / container:
  - runs networked Cursor SDK
  - cannot mint billing-authoritative events directly

Attested sidecar / enclave / confidential VM component:
  - receives raw usage events over local channel
  - signs append-only ledger entries
  - holds short-lived Stigmer reporting key released only after attestation
```

But note the weakness: if the untrusted parent can choose what to send to the enclave, the enclave may only sign lies. To be billing-authoritative, the trusted component must either observe the true usage itself or receive true usage from Cursor/provider. A sidecar that merely signs runner-provided usage improves key protection and tamper-evident logging, but not measurement truth.

For Stigmer cloud mode, the better TEE target is a **confidential VM that runs the whole meter-carrying runner**, not a tiny enclave next to an untrusted runner.

## 4.3 Commitment schemes

Commitments are insufficient by themselves.

A commitment like:

```text
commitment = H(run_id || turn_id || usage || nonce)
```

only proves the runner did not change that exact value later. It does not prove the value was true. A malicious runner can commit to zero usage before the run starts.

Where commitments help:

| Use                                                              | Value    |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------- | -------- |
| Bind provisional runner report to later Admin API reconciliation | Useful   |
| Detect post-hoc mutation of runner logs                          | Useful   |
| Prevent price-dependent manipulation                             | Marginal |
| Make self-reported usage true                                    | No       |

Use commitments as part of a tamper-evident event log, not as a trust anchor.

## 4.4 HMAC with server-issued key

This is commonly proposed and often wrong.

If the server gives the runner an HMAC key and the runner signs usage:

```text
HMAC(k_runner, usage_event)
```

then any user who controls the runner can use the key to sign fake usage. This only authenticates that “someone with this runner credential sent the event,” not that the event is correct.

It is still useful for:

| Purpose                             | Useful? |
| ----------------------------------- | ------- |
| Prevent random third-party spoofing | Yes     |
| Bind reports to a session           | Yes     |
| Prevent local user tampering        | No      |
| Billing authority                   | No      |

# 5. Statistical and behavioral detection

Statistical detection should be treated as a **fraud-control layer**, not the source of truth.

## 5.1 Recommended anomaly features

For each Cursor run/turn, store:

| Feature                                          | Why it matters                   |
| ------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------- |
| Runner-reported input/output/cache tokens        | Baseline claim                   |
| Admin API matched token buckets                  | Settlement comparison            |
| Prompt byte length / approximate tokenizer count | Lower-bound sanity check         |
| Output byte length / tokenizer estimate          | Lower-bound sanity check         |
| Duration                                         | Output/reasoning rough correlate |
| Time to first token                              | Model/load correlate             |
| Model, mode, maxMode                             | Price and distribution segment   |
| Tool-call count                                  | Workload complexity              |
| File diff size                                   | Coding-agent workload proxy      |
| Repo size / context files count                  | Input-token proxy                |
| Number of Cursor turns                           | Aggregation                      |
| User/workspace historical residual               | Fraud signal                     |
| Runner version / commit SHA                      | Build-specific drift             |
| Daytona cloud vs local OSS                       | Trust segment                    |

## 5.2 Detection methods

| Method                            | Use                                                                       |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Robust z-score / MAD on residuals | Detect users whose runner reports are consistently low versus Admin API.  |
| EWMA / CUSUM                      | Detect sudden behavior changes after a runner upgrade/fork.               |
| Isolation Forest                  | Detect unusual multi-feature patterns without labels.                     |
| Peer cohort comparison            | Compare by model, task type, repo size, prompt length, harness mode.      |
| Bayesian confidence score         | Combine fuzzy matching signals into settlement confidence.                |
| Sequential probability ratio test | Decide when repeated small discrepancies become statistically meaningful. |
| Rule-based lower bounds           | Output text cannot be 2,000 tokens if reported output is 10 tokens.       |
| Cost drift by account/day         | Aggregate Cursor Admin API total cents minus Stigmer provisional totals.  |

AWS Cost Anomaly Detection is a useful precedent because it uses machine learning to detect anomalous spend patterns while accounting for seasonality and growth. ([AWS Documentation][7]) AppsFlyer is a useful fraud precedent because it combines machine learning, behavioral analysis, real-time blocking, and post-attribution detection. ([AppsFlyer Support][16])

## 5.3 Honeypot / calibration turns

Use only in Stigmer-controlled cloud mode, and do not rely on secrecy.

Examples:

| Canary                                                         | Expected signal                                |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Server-injected tiny prompt with known approximate token count | Runner must report nonzero input/output.       |
| Synthetic low-cost Cursor turn at session start                | Checks instrumentation path.                   |
| Random audit mode                                              | Forces extra reconciliation for selected runs. |
| Shadow tokenizer lower bound                                   | Flags impossible under-reporting.              |

Limitations:

1. Token counts depend on model/provider tokenizer and hidden formatting.
2. Prompt caching can alter cost buckets.
3. OSS users can inspect/remove canaries.
4. Canaries add cost and latency.
5. They catch sloppy fraud, not sophisticated fraud.

Use canaries to update a fraud score, not to settle billing.

# 6. Recommended Stigmer architecture

## 6.1 Event model

Create a usage event ledger with immutable append semantics.

```ts
type UsageTrustLevel =
  | "BILLING_AUTHORITY"
  | "PROVIDER_SETTLED"
  | "ATTESTED_RUNNER"
  | "SERVER_OBSERVED"
  | "DISPLAY_ONLY";

type UsageSettlementStatus =
  | "ESTIMATED"
  | "MATCHED"
  | "SETTLED"
  | "DISPUTED"
  | "WRITTEN_OFF"
  | "REQUIRES_VERIFIED_MODE";

type UsageEvent = {
  eventId: string;
  runId: string;
  turnId?: string;
  workspaceId: string;
  userId: string;
  harness: "langgraph" | "cursor";
  source: "stigmer_proxy" | "cursor_runner" | "cursor_admin_api" | "cursor_receipt" | "attested_runner";
  trustLevel: UsageTrustLevel;
  settlementStatus: UsageSettlementStatus;

  model?: string;
  provider?: "cursor" | "openai" | "anthropic";
  startedAt?: string;
  endedAt?: string;
  observedAt: string;

  usage?: {
    inputTokens?: number;
    outputTokens?: number;
    cacheReadTokens?: number;
    cacheWriteTokens?: number;
    totalTokens?: number;
  };

  cost?: {
    estimatedCents?: number;
    providerCents?: number;
    billableCents?: number;
    currency: "USD";
  };

  correlation?: {
    cursorUserEmail?: string;
    cursorApiKeyName?: string;
    cursorUsageEventTimestamp?: string;
    cursorAdminEventHash?: string;
    confidence?: number;
    matchedAdminEventIds?: string[];
  };

  integrity?: {
    runnerVersion?: string;
    runnerCommitSha?: string;
    sessionNonce?: string;
    previousEventHash?: string;
    eventHash?: string;
    runnerSignature?: string; // anti-spoofing only, not proof
    attestationDocument?: string;
    receiptJws?: string;
  };
};
```

## 6.2 Ledger rules

| Source                                   | Trust                                     | Billing rule                                |
| ---------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Native proxy                             | `BILLING_AUTHORITY`                       | Final debit immediately or near-immediately |
| Cursor runner `onDelta`                  | `DISPLAY_ONLY`                            | Show estimate; create provisional hold only |
| Cursor Admin API matched high confidence | `PROVIDER_SETTLED`                        | Finalize debit                              |
| Cursor Admin API account/day aggregate   | `PROVIDER_SETTLED` at aggregate           | Settle workspace/day if per-run ambiguous   |
| Future Cursor signed receipt             | `BILLING_AUTHORITY` or `PROVIDER_SETTLED` | Finalize per run                            |
| Future attested cloud runner             | `ATTESTED_RUNNER`                         | Finalize depending on attestation strength  |

## 6.3 Reconciliation algorithm

For every Cursor execution:

1. Ingest runner-reported turn-ended events immediately.
2. Compute estimated cost with current pricing table.
3. Create provisional ledger entry.
4. Poll Cursor Admin API by time window and email/API identity.
5. Generate candidate matches.
6. Score candidates.
7. If confidence ≥ threshold, settle per run.
8. If confidence is medium, settle at workspace/hour or workspace/day.
9. If discrepancy exceeds threshold, mark disputed and update risk score.
10. If repeated discrepancies, require verified cloud mode or suspend Cursor usage.

Example confidence score:

```text
score =
  0.30 * email_or_key_match
+ 0.20 * time_overlap_score
+ 0.15 * model_match
+ 0.10 * maxMode_match
+ 0.20 * token_shape_similarity
+ 0.05 * cost_similarity
```

Settlement thresholds:

| Confidence | Action                                                     |
| ---------: | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
|     ≥ 0.90 | Per-run settled                                            |
|  0.70–0.90 | Probable match; settle if aggregate drift is small         |
|  0.40–0.70 | Do not per-run settle; include in aggregate reconciliation |
|     < 0.40 | Keep estimated; flag if material                           |

## 6.4 Product UX

Use clear labels:

| UI label                | Meaning                                     |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| **Estimated**           | Runner-reported; not provider verified      |
| **Reconciling**         | Waiting for Cursor Admin API settlement     |
| **Settled**             | Matched to provider-side usage              |
| **Adjusted**            | Final provider usage differed from estimate |
| **Unverified**          | Local OSS runner or low-confidence report   |
| **Requires cloud mode** | Account exceeded trust/risk threshold       |

Cloudflare’s distinction between informational notifications and invoice-grade billing is the right UX precedent. ([Cloudflare Docs][19]) Ad-fraud and app-monetization systems also normalize estimated vs final values and post-hoc deductions/credits. ([AppsFlyer Support][16])

# 7. Practical phased plan

## Phase 0 — This week: stop showing `$0.00`

**Goal:** honest UX, no false authority.

Implement:

1. Capture Cursor SDK `onDelta` `turn-ended.usage`.
2. Send to Stigmer billing ingestion with `trustLevel = DISPLAY_ONLY`.
3. Show usage and cost as **Estimated**.
4. Do not mark as final billable usage.
5. Store raw runner events in append-only ledger.
6. Add session nonce and event hash chain to prevent accidental duplication and crude tampering.

Do not wait for perfect tamper resistance. Showing `$0.00` is worse than showing “Estimated $0.14.”

## Phase 1 — 1–2 weeks: Admin API reconciliation

**Goal:** provider-side settlement, even if not perfect per-run.

Implement:

1. Poll Cursor `/teams/filtered-usage-events`.
2. Match by email/API identity, time window, model, maxMode, token shape.
3. Store Cursor Admin API event snapshots.
4. Build confidence scoring.
5. Update UI from **Estimated** to **Settled** or **Adjusted**.
6. Reconcile at workspace/day when per-run matching is ambiguous.
7. Create dashboard: estimated vs settled drift by workspace/user/runner version.

Cursor’s Admin API gives the necessary token buckets and total cents for usage events, even though it lacks your ideal correlation keys. ([Cursor Documentation][3])

## Phase 2 — 2–4 weeks: reduce attribution ambiguity

**Goal:** make reconciliation much more deterministic.

Implement:

1. Dedicated Cursor API key / service account per Stigmer workspace.
2. If feasible, dedicated key per workspace + execution lane.
3. Add Cursor API key name/email into Stigmer run metadata.
4. Prevent high-concurrency jobs from sharing one Cursor identity where possible.
5. Use Cursor spend limits defensively if available in your plan/API surface.

Cursor’s API-key metadata endpoint returning key name and user email is useful here. ([Cursor Documentation][6])

## Phase 3 — 1–2 months: fraud/risk controls

**Goal:** trust-but-verify with consequences.

Implement:

1. Risk score per workspace/user.
2. Drift detection:

   * runner estimate vs Cursor Admin API settlement;
   * estimated cents vs provider cents;
   * local OSS vs Daytona cloud.
3. Canary/calibration turns in Stigmer cloud mode.
4. Rate-limited trust:

   * first $X/day can be provisional;
   * above threshold require cloud mode or settlement before continuing.
5. Policy:

   * repeated under-reporting → downgrade to display-only;
   * high-value workloads → verified cloud mode;
   * severe mismatch → freeze Cursor harness for that workspace pending review.

This mirrors fraud-heavy markets: AppsFlyer and Google use real-time detection, post-attribution review, credits/adjustments, and blocking/penalties rather than pretending every first-party signal is final. ([AppsFlyer Support][16])

## Phase 4 — Vendor ask: Cursor signed usage receipts

**Goal:** make Cursor harness billing-authoritative without proxying Cursor transport.

Ask Cursor for:

1. `externalRunId` parameter in SDK / Cloud Agent creation.
2. Per-turn and per-run usage receipt.
3. Cursor-signed JWS or signed webhook.
4. Published JWKS or HMAC secret rotation.
5. Receipt lookup endpoint:

   * `GET /usage-receipts/{receiptId}`
   * `GET /runs/{cursorRunId}/usage`
6. Include token buckets, total cents, model, user, API key, timestamps, run ID, external ID, receipt ID.
7. Idempotency and replay protection.

This is the most important strategic ask.

## Phase 5 — Longer term: verified execution modes

**Goal:** billing authority for high-value or adversarial environments.

Options:

| Mode                                             | Recommendation                                                                 |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Local OSS runner                                 | Keep display-only or low-limit provisional. Never fully authoritative.         |
| Daytona standard sandbox                         | Medium trust if Stigmer controls image, network, identity, logs.               |
| Daytona + locked image + short-lived credentials | Better.                                                                        |
| Confidential VM / attested runtime               | Best cloud-side option.                                                        |
| Nitro Enclave sidecar only                       | Useful for key protection, but not sufficient if it cannot observe real usage. |
| Full provider proxy                              | Best, but currently blocked by Cursor SDK transport.                           |

Azure Attestation and Nitro Enclaves show that remote attestation can verify platform/binary integrity, but Nitro’s lack of external networking and storage makes a full Cursor SDK loop inside an enclave awkward. ([AWS Documentation][18])

# 8. Direct answers to your research questions

## Can runner-reported Cursor usage be trusted for billing?

Not by itself. It can be used for estimates, dashboards, provisional holds, and anomaly features. It should not be the final billing authority for open-source local runners.

## Can Cursor SDK sign usage locally?

Only if the signing key is protected from the user. A key embedded in OSS code or available to the process is not a trust anchor.

## Could Cursor backend sign usage?

Yes. That is the cleanest solution. The precedent is Apple-signed JWS transaction records and Cursor’s own signed webhook pattern. ([Apple Developer][4])

## Could TEEs solve this?

Partially, in Stigmer-controlled cloud environments. Not for arbitrary local OSS runners. TEEs prove code/environment identity, but the trusted component must observe the measurement or receive signed usage from Cursor. Otherwise it may only sign whatever the untrusted runner tells it.

## Could commitment schemes solve this?

No. They prevent later alteration, not false initial reporting.

## Could anomaly detection solve this?

It can reduce fraud and detect systematic abuse. It cannot make individual self-reported events true.

## Should you bill from Cursor Admin API?

Yes, but with confidence-scored matching and aggregate settlement fallbacks. Treat it as delayed provider settlement.

## Should you show usage today?

Yes. Show runner-reported usage as **Estimated** immediately. The UX need is real, and honest estimated data is better than `$0.00`.

# 9. The recommended Stigmer policy

I would codify this:

> Cursor local/OSS runner usage is display-only until reconciled. Cursor cloud/Stigmer-managed usage may be provisionally debited, but final settlement comes from Cursor Admin API or future Cursor-signed receipts. Native harness proxy usage is billing-authoritative immediately. Accounts with material reconciliation drift are moved to verified cloud mode.

That policy is honest, defensible, and aligned with how serious metered platforms work.

## Final recommendation

Do this now:

1. **Ingest Cursor `onDelta` usage and show “Estimated.”**
2. **Add Cursor Admin API reconciliation.**
3. **Introduce `PROVIDER_SETTLED` as a trust level.**
4. **Use provisional holds, not final debits, for runner-only Cursor usage.**
5. **Create dedicated Cursor identities per workspace/execution lane.**
6. **Build drift/anomaly dashboards from day one.**
7. **Push Cursor hard for signed usage receipts or usage-bearing signed webhooks.**
