## What Was Created

### Demo Story Narrative (376 lines)

Five-part structure:

1. **Narrative framework** — reusable five-beat arc:
   - Beat 1 (Before): The founder's reality with a generic LLM
   - Beat 2 (Teach): Upload domain knowledge → Pillar 1 "Knows Your Business"
   - Beat 3 (Connect): Give tool access → Pillar 2 "Uses Your Tools"
   - Beat 4 (Rules): Configure approvals → Pillar 3 "Asks Before Acting"
   - Beat 5 (After): Agent in production → Foundation "Built for Production"

2. **Primary story: E-commerce platform** — a founder of a multi-vendor e-commerce platform adding AI agents as a product feature for hundreds of merchants. Full narrative with concrete proof interactions at each act.

3. **Capability map** — table connecting each narrative beat to messaging pillar, Stigmer feature, vocabulary-guide term (sales-site column), and polished draft copy blocks.

4. **Variant sketches** — two short scenarios proving the framework generalizes:
   - Property management SaaS (per-building rules, maintenance ticketing, lease approvals)
   - Logistics platform (SLA terms, real-time tracking, cost-based rerouting approvals)

5. **Phase 2 notes** — how the five-beat arc maps to homepage scroll sections and adjacent page structure.

## Decisions Made

1. **Scenario selection**: E-commerce platform chosen over property management, helpdesk, and logistics candidates. Scored highest on audience resonance (platform builder adding AI to their product) and generalizability (every SaaS founder recognizes the pattern). The "overused customer support demo" risk is neutralized by the framing: this is a platform builder shipping agent capabilities as infrastructure, not a support team configuring a chatbot.

2. **Document scope**: Defined as a strategic narrative artifact in `design-decisions/`, not final sales site copy. Phase 2 owns layout, scroll rhythm, visual design, and component structure.

3. **Copy block polish level**: Polished drafts — strong starting points for Phase 2 that read like sales copy but don't pretend to be final (character counts, visual hierarchy, and scroll rhythm are Phase 2 concerns).

4. **Vocabulary enforcement**: Sales-site register terms used throughout the narrative. Technical terms (Skill, MCP Server, ToolApprovalPolicy, Sessions) appear only in the capability map.

5. **Variant sketch scope**: Lightweight by design — scenario + before + transformation table + one-sentence after. They validate the framework, not replace the primary story.
