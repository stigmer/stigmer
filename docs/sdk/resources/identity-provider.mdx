An IdentityProvider represents an external platform's trust relationship with
Stigmer. It configures how Stigmer validates the tokens that platform's users
present on each request, and how their federated identity accounts are created.
Served by the Enterprise and Cloud editions; the open-source server answers
UNIMPLEMENTED.

```yaml
apiVersion: iam.stigmer.ai/v1
kind: IdentityProvider
metadata:
  name: Planton
  slug: planton
  org: planton
spec:
  display_name: "Planton"
  jwks_uri: "https://planton-prod.us.auth0.com/.well-known/jwks.json"
  allowed_issuers: ["https://planton-prod.us.auth0.com/"]
  expected_audience: "https://api.planton.ai/"
  userinfo_endpoint: "https://planton-prod.us.auth0.com/userinfo"
```

### create

Create an identity provider.

The creator's organization owns the identity provider.

On Stigmer Enterprise, a new identity provider is growth the server's license pays for. Once the license and its grace period have ended, a create is refused with FAILED_PRECONDITION carrying a google.rpc.ErrorInfo detail (domain "stigmer.ai"), before anything is written:

- LICENSE_RENEWAL_REQUIRED — the server's license has ended; every identity provider that exists keeps signing people in. No metadata.

A slug another identity provider of the organization holds is refused with ALREADY_EXISTS. A slug another identity provider was renamed away from, which still leads to it for 30 days, is refused with ALREADY_EXISTS carrying a google.rpc.ErrorInfo detail (domain "stigmer.ai"):

- SLUG_RESERVED — another identity provider held the slug until a recent rename, and it still leads there. Metadata: slug.

A create that sets sso_required also needs can_assign_roles on the organization, which only its owners hold (PERMISSION_DENIED otherwise): requiring sign-in through a provider decides how every member gets in. Where the organization's plan decides it, a plan without SSO enforcement refuses it with FAILED_PRECONDITION carrying a google.rpc.ErrorInfo detail (domain "stigmer.ai"):

- PLAN_UPGRADE_REQUIRED — the organization's plan does not include SSO enforcement. Metadata: feature ("sso_enforcement"), org.

### update

Update an identity provider: the fields update_mask names change, every other field keeps its stored value.

On Stigmer Enterprise, changing how people sign in is growth the server's license pays for. Once the license and its grace period have ended, an update is refused with FAILED_PRECONDITION carrying a google.rpc.ErrorInfo detail (domain "stigmer.ai"), before anything is written:

- LICENSE_RENEWAL_REQUIRED — the server's license has ended; the identity provider keeps signing people in as it is, and can still be deleted. No metadata.

An update whose only change is turning sso_required off is never refused this way: an organization may always stop requiring its SSO provider.

An update that changes sso_required, or that changes whom a provider with sso_required on trusts (allowed_issuers, jwks_uri, expected_audience), also needs can_assign_roles on the organization, which only its owners hold (PERMISSION_DENIED otherwise). Turning it on is refused with PLAN_UPGRADE_REQUIRED where the organization's plan does not include SSO enforcement (see create); an organization that already requires it keeps it, and may always turn it off.

resource.metadata.id names the identity provider; apply finds one by slug. The slug changes only through rename, and a mask naming a field another door owns, or a path the identity provider does not have, is refused with INVALID_ARGUMENT naming the path.

An update is refused with ABORTED carrying a google.rpc.ErrorInfo detail (domain "stigmer.ai"):

- ETAG_MISMATCH — resource.metadata.etag is set and the identity provider changed since that etag was read: read it again and make the change again. No metadata. - WRITE_CONFLICT — the identity provider kept changing while the update was being saved: try again. No metadata.

### createAccessMove

Start moving your access in an organization to the account its SSO provider signs you in as.

For someone who worked in an organization under another sign-in (an email login) before the organization connected its SSO provider. The caller, signed in the old way, asks for a ticket; the answer says what the move will do (AccessMoveTicket.preview: the access that moves, the My vault entries it deletes, the automations that stop); the person then signs in through the organization's SSO provider and redeems the ticket with redeemAccessMove. The ticket works once, for ten minutes, for this account and this organization only.

Refused with FAILED_PRECONDITION when the caller holds no access in the organization, is one of its owners (owners keep their own sign-in), or already signed in through an SSO provider; with NOT_FOUND when the organization has no SSO provider (the same copy getSsoProvider answers).

### redeemAccessMove

Redeem an access move ticket: the access it names moves to the caller.

The caller must be signed in through the organization's SSO provider, as the account it made for them; anyone else is refused with PERMISSION_DENIED and the ticket stays usable until it expires. A ticket that is unknown, expired or already redeemed is refused with FAILED_PRECONDITION. A move that stops partway may be redeemed again with the same ticket while it lasts, and completes.

Every change is recorded in the organization's permission history as a move, acting for the caller.

### rename

Rename an identity provider: change its slug, the name people type.

The identity provider keeps its id, so everything that names it keeps working, and every answer names it by the new slug. The old slug keeps leading to the identity provider for reading for 30 days, during which no other identity provider of the organization can take it and this one can take it back; then it is released. A write that names the old slug is refused with SLUG_RENAMED (see apply). A slug another identity provider holds is refused with ALREADY_EXISTS, and one another identity provider was recently renamed away from with SLUG_RESERVED (see create).

Sign-in does not name its slug, so the people it signs in keep signing in.

### IamRole

IamRole defines the roles that can be assigned to principals on resources via IAM policies.

| Value | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| `owner` | Full control of the resource, including deleting it and deciding who has access. |
| `admin` | Administers an organization: its people, settings and resources, short of its owners, its deletion and its private conversations. |
| `member` | Belongs to an organization or a team. |
| `viewer` | Sees the resource and changes nothing. |
| `participant` | A person who takes part in a conversation: reads it and sends messages. |
| `editor` | Editor of a blueprint (an agent): may change its definition and do whatever a viewer can, and may not delete it, change its visibility, publish it on a share link or a channel, or decide who else has access. |
| `user` | May use a shared vault: the runs of a person who holds it use the vault's logins and secrets. |
