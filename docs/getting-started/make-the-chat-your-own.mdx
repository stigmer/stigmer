  Give the chat in your app your brand, your own card when the Agent uses one of
  your app's tools, your own action on its answer, your own bubble for your

## What you'll build

Your customers now run the variance report job inside your product, but the chat
still looks and acts like Stigmer's. On this page you change it, one step at a
time, until it feels like part of your app:

- it wears your brand's colours and font, in light and dark;
- when the Agent wants a person to check a flagged account, it calls one of your
  app's own tools, and your app draws its own account card in the chat, with
  **Looks right** and **Needs a look**. The Agent hears which one they picked;
- the Agent's final answer gets your own **Add to month-end notes** button, next
  to Copy;
- your users' messages are drawn in your own bubble;
- a line in your app's header, outside the chat, says what the Agent is doing.

Each step uses the smallest way that does the job, so everything you don't
change stays Stigmer's: approvals, the order of events, Stop, and keyboard and
screen-reader access.

The moment to watch for: the Agent stops to ask about an account, your own card
asks your user, and the Agent carries on with what they picked.

**Time**: about 20 minutes. **Prerequisites**: the app from
[Put the agent in your app](/docs/getting-started/embed-agent), with its
`server.ts` and `src/App.tsx`. If you also gave each customer an Organization of
their own on the
[last page](/docs/getting-started/one-organization-per-customer), the app keeps
working that way: everything here happens in the browser.

## Wear your brand

The chat's look comes from design tokens, CSS variables named `--stgm-*`. Give
it your colour and font with a theme class. Create `src/brand.css`:

```css title="src/brand.css"
.brand-theme {
  --stgm-primary: oklch(0.5 0.13 165);
  --stgm-primary-foreground: oklch(0.985 0 0);
  --stgm-ring: oklch(0.5 0.1 165);
  --stgm-font-sans: "Avenir Next", "Segoe UI", system-ui, sans-serif;
  font-family: var(--stgm-font-sans);
}

.brand-theme[data-stgm-color-mode="dark"],
[data-stgm-color-mode="dark"] .brand-theme {
  --stgm-primary: oklch(0.78 0.13 165);
  --stgm-primary-foreground: oklch(0.145 0 0);
  --stgm-ring: oklch(0.78 0.1 165);
}
```

Every colour you change gets a dark value too, and every fill keeps its
`-foreground` partner, so text on it stays readable. Then, in `src/App.tsx`,
import the stylesheet and hand the class to `StigmerProvider`:

```tsx no-compile="a run of src/App.tsx, shown whole in the last step"
import "./brand.css";
// …
<StigmerProvider
  client={stigmer}
  className="brand-theme"
  colorMode="system"
```

`colorMode="system"` follows your user's computer, so the dark values show when
it is dark. If your app has its own light and dark switch, pass its value
instead. [Theming](/docs/sdk/theme/theming) has every token and six ready-made
presets.

</Step>

## Draw your own card when the Agent uses your app's tool

Your app can offer the Agent tools of its own. When the Agent calls one, the
conversation waits for your app's answer, and your own card can draw the call
and answer it. Create `src/open-account.tsx`:

```tsx title="src/open-account.tsx"
import { useState } from "react";
import { defineCustomTool } from "@stigmer/client";
import type { ToolCardProps } from "@stigmer/react-sdk/chat";

export const OPEN_ACCOUNT = defineCustomTool({
  name: "open_account",
  description:
    "Show the person one ledger account the report flags, for them to check. Returns what they said: Looks right, or Needs a look.",
  inputSchema: {
    type: "object",
    properties: {
      account: {
        type: "string",
        description: "The account's name, as in the ledger.",
      },
      variance: {
        type: "string",
        description: "How far it is off budget, such as +18%.",
      },
    },
    required: ["account"],
  },
});

export function OpenAccountCard({ call, answer }: ToolCardProps) {
  const [picked, setPicked] = useState<string | null>(null);
  const [error, setError] = useState<string | null>(null);
  const account =
    typeof call.input.account === "string" ? call.input.account : "An account";
  const variance =
    typeof call.input.variance === "string" ? call.input.variance : null;

  async function reply(verdict: string) {
    if (answer === undefined) return;
    setPicked(verdict);
    setError(null);
    try {
      await answer(verdict);
    } catch (failure) {
      setPicked(null);
      setError(
        failure instanceof Error
          ? failure.message
          : "Your answer didn't reach Stigmer.",
      );
    }
  }

  return (
    <section
      aria-label={`Account ${account}`}
      style={{ display: "grid", gap: 8, padding: 12 }}
    >
      <strong>{account}</strong>
      {variance !== null && <span>{variance} against budget</span>}
      {call.result !== undefined ? (
        <span>Checked: {call.result.text}</span>
      ) : answer !== undefined ? (
        <div style={{ display: "flex", gap: 8 }}>
          <button
            type="button"
            disabled={picked !== null}
            onClick={() => void reply("Looks right")}
          >
            Looks right
          </button>
          <button
            type="button"
            disabled={picked !== null}
            onClick={() => void reply("Needs a look")}
          >
            Needs a look
          </button>
        </div>
      ) : null}
      {error !== null && <span role="alert">{error}</span>}
    </section>
  );
}
```

- `defineCustomTool` declares the tool: its name, what it is for, and the input
  the Agent must give it. The description is what the Agent reads to decide when
  to call it.
- The card reads the call (`call.input`, and `call.result` once it is answered).
  `answer` sends your user's pick back as the tool's result. It is there while
  the call waits, and only for the conversation's owner: someone only watching
  sees the account and no buttons.
- `answer` can fail, for example when the network drops, so the card holds its
  buttons while the answer is on its way and shows the error if it doesn't
  arrive.

In `src/App.tsx`, register the card for the tool's name, once, on the provider,
and offer the tool to the conversation:

```tsx no-compile="a run of src/App.tsx, shown whole in the last step"
import {
  AgentChat,
  StigmerProvider,
  type ToolCards,
} from "@stigmer/react-sdk/chat";
// …
import { OPEN_ACCOUNT, OpenAccountCard } from "./open-account";
// …
const TOOL_CARDS: ToolCards = { open_account: OpenAccountCard };
const CUSTOM_TOOLS = [OPEN_ACCOUNT];
// …
toolCards = { TOOL_CARDS };
// …
customTools = { CUSTOM_TOOLS };
```

Your card goes inside Stigmer's step for the call. The step's line stays ours
and keeps saying what is happening, an approval the call needs stays ours, and
if your card throws, ours draws instead. Define cards at the top level of a
module, as here, not inside a component.

The same `toolCards` map takes a card for a tool of a server you connected to
the Agent, keyed by its name as the Agent sees it, `mcp__<server>__<tool>`:

```tsx no-compile="a fragment beside the steps; InvoiceCard is yours"
const TOOL_CARDS: ToolCards = { mcp__billing__create_invoice: InvoiceCard };
```

## Put your own action on the Agent's answer

Add a button to the Agent's final answer that hands it to the rest of your app.
Create `src/month-end-notes.tsx`, a small list of notes with the action that
adds to it:

```tsx title="src/month-end-notes.tsx"
import { useCallback, useState } from "react";
import type { MessageAction, MessageInfo } from "@stigmer/react-sdk/chat";

export function useMonthEndNotes() {
  const [saved, setSaved] = useState<readonly string[]>([]);
  const actionsFor = useCallback(
    (message: MessageInfo): readonly MessageAction[] => {
      if (!message.isFinalAnswer) return [];
      return [
        {
          id: "add-to-notes",
          label: "Add to month-end notes",
          icon: <NotesIcon />,
          onAction: () => setSaved((notes) => [...notes, message.text]),
        },
      ];
    },
    [],
  );
  return { saved, actionsFor };
}

export function MonthEndNotes({ saved }: { saved: readonly string[] }) {
  return (
    <aside
      aria-label="Month-end notes"
      style={{ padding: 16, overflow: "auto" }}
    >
      <h2 style={{ fontSize: 16 }}>Month-end notes</h2>
      {saved.length === 0 ? (
        <p>Nothing added yet.</p>
      ) : (
        <ul>
          {saved.map((note, index) => (
            <li key={index}>{note}</li>
          ))}
        </ul>
      )}
    </aside>
  );
}

function NotesIcon() {
  return (
    <svg
      viewBox="0 0 16 16"
      width="16"
      height="16"
      fill="none"
      stroke="currentColor"
      strokeWidth="1.5"
      aria-hidden="true"
    >
      <path d="M4 2.5h8v11H4zM6.5 6h3M6.5 8.5h3" />
    </svg>
  );
}
```

`actionsFor` is told about each message: whose it is, its text, and whether it
is the final answer of a finished turn. It returns the actions for that message,
here one, only on the final answer. In `src/App.tsx`, pass it to the chat and
show the list beside it:

```tsx no-compile="a run of src/App.tsx, shown whole in the last step"
import { MonthEndNotes, useMonthEndNotes } from "./month-end-notes";
// …
const notes = useMonthEndNotes();
// …
messageActions={notes.actionsFor}
// …
<MonthEndNotes saved={notes.saved} />
```

An action with an icon sits beside Copy, named by its label. Up to two do; any
more go into one **More actions** menu. Every action is reachable by keyboard,
and none takes a key from your page.

</Step>

## Draw your users' messages your way

To change a part of the conversation itself, swap it for your own component. A
swap draws the cases it changes and hands every other case back to Stigmer's.
Create `src/person-bubble.tsx`:

```tsx title="src/person-bubble.tsx"
import {
  MessageEntry,
  type MessageEntryProps,
  type MessageThreadSlots,
} from "@stigmer/react-sdk/chat";

function PersonBubble(props: MessageEntryProps) {
  if (props.message.kind !== "user") return <MessageEntry {...props} />;
  return (
    <div
      className="person-bubble"
      style={{
        marginLeft: "auto",
        maxWidth: "75%",
        padding: "8px 14px",
        borderRadius: 18,
        background: "var(--stgm-primary)",
        color: "var(--stgm-primary-foreground)",
      }}
    >
      {props.files?.map((file) => (
        <small
          key={file.fileId ?? file.filename}
          style={{ display: "block", opacity: 0.85 }}
        >
          {file.filename ?? "Attached file"}
        </small>
      ))}
      {props.message.text}
    </div>
  );
}

export const THREAD_SLOTS: MessageThreadSlots = { MessageEntry: PersonBubble };
```

The bubble reads your brand's own tokens, so it follows light and dark with the
rest. Every other message goes back to `MessageEntry`. Handing back loads that
part with your page, before the chat draws, where the chat alone loads it with
the conversation. In `src/App.tsx`:

```tsx no-compile="a run of src/App.tsx, shown whole in the last step"
import { THREAD_SLOTS } from "./person-bubble";
// …
threadSlots = { THREAD_SLOTS };
```

Approvals, to-do lists, plans and the other parts swap the same way;
[Theming](/docs/sdk/theme/theming) lists them. Define the swaps at the top level
of a module, as here.

</Step>

## Show what the Agent is doing in your own header

The chat says what the Agent is doing above its message box. Your app can say it
anywhere else too, read from the same conversation. Create
`src/agent-status.tsx`:

```tsx title="src/agent-status.tsx"
import { activityPhrase, describeTurnActivity } from "@stigmer/client";
import { useSessionConversation } from "@stigmer/react-sdk/headless";

export function AgentStatus({
  sessionId,
  org,
}: {
  sessionId: string | null;
  org: string;
}) {
  const { activeTurn } = useSessionConversation(sessionId, org);
  if (activeTurn === null) return <span>Ready</span>;
  return (
    <span>
      {activityPhrase(describeTurnActivity(activeTurn, Date.now()).activity)}
    </span>
  );
}
```

`useSessionConversation` reads the conversation the chat shows: its turns, the
one at work, its files. `describeTurnActivity` and `activityPhrase` put the turn
at work in the same words the chat uses, such as "Waiting for the app" while
your card waits for your user. In `src/App.tsx`, keep the conversation's ID that
`onSessionChange` hands you and put the line in your header:

```tsx no-compile="a run of src/App.tsx, shown whole in the last step"
import { AgentStatus } from "./agent-status";
// …
const [sessionId, setSessionId] = useState<string | null>(null);
// …
<AgentStatus sessionId={sessionId} org={org} />;
// …
onSessionChange = { setSessionId };
```

To draw the whole conversation yourself, from the same hook, see
[Build a chat from the hooks](/docs/sdk/react-core/build-a-chat).

## Run it

Here is `src/App.tsx` with every step in place. Replace yours with it:

```tsx title="src/App.tsx" no-compile="imports this page's own files; the embedding tutorial's test builds the whole app, strict"
import { useState } from "react";
import { Stigmer, createTokenCache } from "@stigmer/client";
import {
  AgentChat,
  StigmerProvider,
  type ToolCards,
} from "@stigmer/react-sdk/chat";
import "@stigmer/react-sdk/styles.css";
import "./brand.css";
import { AgentStatus } from "./agent-status";
import { MonthEndNotes, useMonthEndNotes } from "./month-end-notes";
import { OPEN_ACCOUNT, OpenAccountCard } from "./open-account";
import { THREAD_SLOTS } from "./person-bubble";

const org = import.meta.env.VITE_STIGMER_ORG;
// Set when each customer has an Organization of their own; otherwise the Agent is in yours.
const agentOrg = import.meta.env.VITE_STIGMER_AGENT_ORG ?? org;

const tokens = createTokenCache(async () => {
  const res = await fetch("/api/stigmer-token");
  return res.json();
});

const stigmer = new Stigmer({
  baseUrl: import.meta.env.VITE_STIGMER_URL ?? "https://api.stigmer.ai",
  getAccessToken: tokens.getAccessToken,
});

const TOOL_CARDS: ToolCards = { open_account: OpenAccountCard };
const CUSTOM_TOOLS = [OPEN_ACCOUNT];

export default function App() {
  const [sessionId, setSessionId] = useState<string | null>(null);
  const notes = useMonthEndNotes();
  return (
    <StigmerProvider
      client={stigmer}
      className="brand-theme"
      colorMode="system"
      toolCards={TOOL_CARDS}
    >
      <div
        style={{
          display: "grid",
          gridTemplateColumns: "1fr 18rem",
          gridTemplateRows: "auto 1fr",
          height: "100vh",
        }}
      >
        <header
          style={{
            gridColumn: "1 / -1",
            display: "flex",
            justifyContent: "space-between",
            padding: "12px 16px",
          }}
        >
          <strong>Month-end close</strong>
          <AgentStatus sessionId={sessionId} org={org} />
        </header>
        <main style={{ minHeight: 0 }}>
          <AgentChat
            agent={`${agentOrg}/variance-reporter`}
            org={org}
            customTools={CUSTOM_TOOLS}
            messageActions={notes.actionsFor}
            threadSlots={THREAD_SLOTS}
            onSessionChange={setSessionId}
          />
        </main>
        <MonthEndNotes saved={notes.saved} />
      </div>
    </StigmerProvider>
  );
}
```

Start your backend, then the app, in two terminals:

```bash
npx tsx server.ts
```

```bash
VITE_STIGMER_ORG=$STIGMER_ORG npm run dev
```

If you gave each customer an Organization, start the app with both, as on the
last page: this file reads both, as yours did.

Open `http://localhost:5173`. The chat wears your colour, and switches to its
dark values when your computer is dark. Attach `ledger.csv`, send "Review this
quarter's ledger and show me any account that looks off." in your own bubble,
and watch your header:

1. The Agent reads the ledger and calls `open_account` for an account off
   budget. Your card shows the account under Stigmer's step, and your header
   says **Waiting for the app**.
2. Pick **Looks right** or **Needs a look**. The card says what you picked, and
   the Agent carries on with it.
3. When the Agent's answer arrives, choose **Add to month-end notes** next to
   Copy. The answer appears in your notes.

The Agent decides when to call your tool from its description and your message,
so if it doesn't call it, ask it to show you the account that is furthest off
budget.

## What just happened

The chat became part of your app at five levels, each the smallest one that did
the job:

- **Tokens** gave it your colour and font.
- **Your app's own tool**, declared with `defineCustomTool`, let the Agent ask
  your app something in the middle of its work. Your **tool card** drew the call
  and answered it.
- **Your action** put your app's next step on the Agent's answer.
- **A swapped part** drew your users' messages your way and handed the rest back
  to Stigmer's.
- **The conversation hook** let your header read what the chat shows.

Your app can also answer from somewhere else than a card: hear the call with
`onCustomToolUse` on `AgentChat` and answer from your own panel
([Embed an Agent](/docs/guides/channels/embed-an-agent#the-apps-own-tools)), or
answer from your backend with the TypeScript SDK's `answerCustomToolUse`
([Answer a call of your app's own tool](/docs/sdk/streaming#answer-a-call-of-your-apps-own-tool)).

## What stays Stigmer's, and why

Some things look the way you want but work the same in every app, so your users
can trust them:

- **What an approval shows and records.** You can draw the approval card your
  way, but what it shows the person and the decision it records are Stigmer's.
- **The order of events.** The conversation shows what happened in the order it
  happened, in every app and when it is opened again later.
- **Stop.** Your user can always stop the Agent.
- **Keyboard and screen-reader access.** Stigmer's parts are reached and named
  the same way in every app. Your parts sit inside them, so give your buttons
  names, as the card and the action here do.

## Everything your own

When none of this goes far enough, draw the whole screen from the hooks
([Build a chat from the hooks](/docs/sdk/react-core/build-a-chat)), or skip
React altogether with the [TypeScript SDK](/docs/sdk/typescript), the same SDK
your backend already uses to sign your users in.
