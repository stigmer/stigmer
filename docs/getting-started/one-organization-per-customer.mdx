## What you'll build

On the last page your users ran the variance report job inside your app, all of
them in your own Organization. Here each of your customers gets an Organization
of their own under yours, a **child organization**. A customer's people sign in
to it, and their conversations, files and keys stay inside it: another
customer's people can't see them.

The moment to watch for: your user runs the same job, and the conversation
belongs to their customer's Organization, not yours.

**Time**: about 15 minutes. **Prerequisites**:

- The app and backend from
  [Put the agent in your app](/docs/getting-started/embed-agent), with the
  `report-backend` service account's key as `STIGMER_API_KEY` and your
  Organization's slug as `STIGMER_ORG`.
- Child organizations, which Stigmer Cloud has on a plan that includes them, and
  Stigmer Enterprise has as far as its license allows.
  [Pricing](https://stigmer.ai/pricing) says which plans include them, and
  [Open source, Enterprise and Cloud](/docs/guides/self-hosting/cloud-vs-self-hosted)
  compares the editions.

<Callout type="info">
  The open-source server holds one Organization. There, keep each customer's
  keys in a vault of their own, and have your backend start each customer's
  conversations with that vault: [Keep your customers'
  keys](/docs/guides/authentication/customer-keys) shows how.
</Callout>

<Steps>
<Step>

## Share the Agent with your customers

Your customers' Organizations run the Agent you already have. In the console,
open **Library → Agents → variance-reporter**. Its visibility reads
**Organization**: click it to open **Manage access**, choose **Child
organizations** under **General access**, and confirm. Everyone in every child
organization can then use it, including the ones you create later, and nobody
else can. Each customer runs it inside their own Organization.

</Step>
<Step>

## Create the customer's organization

When a customer signs up for your product, your backend creates their
Organization under yours. Creating an Organization decides who belongs where, so
Stigmer refuses it from a service account, and a leaked key can't add any. An
admin's own key does it. Signed in to the CLI as an admin, make a key limited to
your Organization, which works there and in the Organizations under it and
nowhere else:

```bash
stigmer apikey create --name onboarding --bound-org <your-org>
export STIGMER_ADMIN_KEY=<the key>
```

Create `onboard-customer.ts` in your app's folder:

```typescript title="onboard-customer.ts"
import { createNodeClient } from "@stigmer/client/node";

const stigmer = createNodeClient({
  baseUrl: process.env.STIGMER_URL ?? "https://api.stigmer.ai",
  apiKey: process.env.STIGMER_ADMIN_KEY!,
});

const customer = await stigmer.organization.create({
  name: "Northwind Books",
  slug: "northwind-books",
  parentOrg: process.env.STIGMER_ORG!,
  externalId: "cust-4411",
});

console.log(`Created ${customer.metadata!.slug}`);
```

`externalId` is your own ID for the customer, so you can find their Organization
later without storing its slug. Run it:

```bash
npx tsx onboard-customer.ts
```

Your backend keeps signing people in with the service account's key: an admin of
your Organization is an admin of each of its children, so it can mint tokens in
the customer's Organization too.

</Step>
<Step>

## Sign the customer's people in to it

In `server.ts`, mint each user's token into their customer's Organization
instead of yours. Change the `org` the mint names:

```typescript
const { accessToken, expiresAt } = await stigmer.tokens.mintUserToken({
  org: "northwind-books",
  userId: user.id,
  userEmail: user.email,
  userName: user.name,
});
```

In your product, `org` is the Organization of the customer this user belongs to.
The token works there and nowhere else. The first mint for a user creates their
account in that Organization, with the sign-in role from your own Organization's
embedding settings.

In `src/App.tsx`, the conversation now lives in the customer's Organization,
while the Agent still lives in yours. Read both:

```tsx
const org = import.meta.env.VITE_STIGMER_ORG;
const agentOrg = import.meta.env.VITE_STIGMER_AGENT_ORG;
```

and name the Agent by your Organization:

```tsx
initialAgentRef={{ org: agentOrg, slug: "variance-reporter" }}
```

</Step>
<Step>

## Run the job as the customer's user

Restart your backend, and start the app with both Organizations:

```bash
npx tsx server.ts
```

```bash
VITE_STIGMER_ORG=northwind-books VITE_STIGMER_AGENT_ORG=$STIGMER_ORG npm run dev
```

Open `http://localhost:5173`, attach `ledger.csv`, and send "Review this
quarter's ledger." The report comes back as before. This time the conversation
and its files belong to Northwind Books' Organization.

</Step>
</Steps>

## What just happened

Your customer has a place of their own in Stigmer. Their people sign in to it
through your backend, the Agent you shared runs inside it, and what they upload
and get back stays there. A second customer gets a second Organization, and
neither sees the other's conversations.

Your Organization's admins manage every customer's Organization, and your
customers' people see only what you share with them. One thing stays private
even from your admins: each person's own conversations.
