title: Bring Your Own OAuth App
description:
  Register your Organization's own OAuth app for the addresses it signs in to,
  for a vendor Stigmer has no app for, tighter scope control, or your own
  branding.
---

Signing in to a tool needs an OAuth client the vendor knows. For many tools
Stigmer finds one on its own: a built-in app the deployment ships (GitHub), or a
client it registers with the tool's own login server. When neither works, or
when your Organization wants its own, an admin adds a **login app**: an OAuth
app your Organization registered with the vendor, listed against the addresses
it signs in to. Stigmer calls this **Bring Your Own OAuth App**.

<Callout type="info">
  This guide assumes you've read the [OAuth for
  tools](/docs/guides/integrations/oauth-for-tools) guide. A login app changes
  whose OAuth client signs people in. It does not change how the sign-in itself
  works.
</Callout>

## When to bring your own app

Three situations call for one:

1. **Nothing else can sign in at the address.** Some vendors do not let a client
   register itself, and Stigmer has no built-in app for them. A sign-in there is
   refused with "add a login app for `ADDRESS` in Settings, or paste a token"
   until an admin adds one.
2. **You need tighter control.** Your Organization may require narrower scopes,
   a branded consent page, or credentials held under your own vendor account.
3. **The vendor has not approved Stigmer's app yet.** Vendors like Slack and
   Figma review third-party apps before end users can authorize them. Your own
   registration lets your Organization sign in meanwhile.

Your Organization's app comes first: once it lists an address, every sign-in in
your Organization at that address uses it, ahead of Stigmer's built-in app and
of the tool's own login server.
[OAuth architecture](/docs/guides/integrations/oauth-architecture#a-login-is-found-by-its-address)
gives the full order.

## Set up your own OAuth app

1. **Register an app with the vendor.** In the vendor's developer portal (for
   example, [Slack's OAuth app page](https://api.slack.com/apps)), create an app
   and give it your deployment's callback URL: the console's address followed by
   `/auth/oauth/callback`. For the desktop app, also add the same URL ending in
   `?source=desktop`, and `http://127.0.0.1/auth/oauth/callback` if the vendor
   accepts loopback addresses.
2. **Open Settings > Configuration > OAuth Apps** and create an app. Enter the
   provider's name, the **Client ID** and **Client Secret** from the vendor's
   portal, its authorization and token URLs, the scopes to request, and the
   **Addresses** the app signs in to: the URL of each tool
   (`https://mcp.slack.com/mcp`) or a Git host (`github.com`).
3. **Save.** The next sign-in at any of those addresses, by anyone in your
   Organization, goes through your app, and the vendor's consent page shows your
   app's name.

The same app as a definition you can keep in a repository and `stigmer apply`:

```yaml validate-as="OAuthApp"
apiVersion: iam.stigmer.ai/v1
kind: OAuthApp
metadata:
  name: Slack
  org: acme
spec:
  provider: Slack
  client_id: "1234567890.abcdef"
  client_secret: "replace-with-your-secret"
  authorization_url: "https://slack.com/oauth/v2/authorize"
  token_url: "https://slack.com/api/oauth.v2.access"
  scopes: ["channels:read", "chat:write"]
  scope_parameter_name: user_scope
  addresses: ["https://mcp.slack.com/mcp"]
```

An address belongs to one app in an Organization: listing an address another app
already lists is refused, naming the address. Addresses are written the way a
vault writes them: scheme and host lowercased, a default port and one trailing
slash dropped.

Set `userinfo_url` to the vendor's account endpoint (an OpenID Connect UserInfo
endpoint, or a vendor's own such as GitHub's `https://api.github.com/user`) and
each saved login is described by the account that signed in, such as "Slack
@ana", instead of by its host.

## What changes after setup

- **New sign-ins** at the app's addresses use your app's client, by everyone in
  your Organization.
- **Existing sign-ins** keep the client they were made with and keep renewing
  with it. Sign in again to move one to your app.
- **The app's secret** is used only for the sign-ins made with it: a renewal
  sends it only while the app's client ID and token URL are the ones the sign-in
  recorded.

## Change or remove an app

Edit or delete the app in **Settings > Configuration > OAuth Apps**. Removing an
address from an app, or deleting the app, sends the next sign-in at that address
back to Stigmer's built-in app or the tool's own login server.

<Callout type="warn">
  Changing an app's client ID or token URL, or deleting the app, stops the
  sign-ins made with it from renewing: their next renewal goes out without your
  app's secret, and a vendor that requires one refuses it. Each affected person
  sees **Re-auth needed** and signs in again with a single click.
</Callout>
