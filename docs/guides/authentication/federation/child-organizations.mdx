---
title: Child organizations
description:
  Give each of your customers an isolated Organization under yours, administer
  it from your Organization, and route your customers' sign-ins to it.
---

If your platform serves several customers, and each customer needs its own
isolated set of Agents, Skills, Sessions and access controls, you create one
**child organization** per customer under your own **parent organization**. A
child organization is a full tenant boundary: what one child holds is invisible
to the people of another child.

## When you need this

You already have a working
[Federation Issuer](/docs/guides/authentication/federation/register-federation-issuer),
or a backend that
[mints user tokens](/docs/guides/authentication/product-users), and you want to:

- give each of your customers its own Agents, Skills, Plugins and Sessions
- mirror your platform's customers in Stigmer, one Organization each, and find
  each by your own customer id
- administer those Organizations from your own, as their admins (their Agents,
  Skills, settings, members and access), and see their billing
- share a catalog of Agents, Skills and Plugins with every customer, including
  customers you add later

## Prerequisites

- A **Stigmer Organization** of your own, the parent (for example, `acme`)
- An **API key** of an admin of that Organization. A key
  [limited to the Organization](/docs/guides/authentication/overview) works too:
  a key limited to the parent creates its children and works in each of them as
  the account behind it would.
- For sign-in through your own auth provider, a **registered Federation Issuer**
  in the parent (see
  [Register a Federation Issuer](/docs/guides/authentication/federation/register-federation-issuer))

A child organization records two facts, both fixed when it is created:

| Field        | Purpose                                                                                                                      |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `parentOrg`  | The parent organization, by id or slug. Creating a child needs admin rights in the parent.                                   |
| `externalId` | Your own identifier for this customer (`cust-4411`). Optional, and unique among the parent's children. Requires `parentOrg`. |

Child organizations are one level deep: a child cannot have children of its own,
and a child is never moved to another parent.

Nobody owns a new child organization. Your Organization's admins are its admins,
and grant its people their roles, as the next sections show.

| Error                         | When                                                                                    |
| ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `PERMISSION_DENIED` (`403`)   | You are not an admin of the parent, or no Organization has that id or slug              |
| `ALREADY_EXISTS` (`409`)      | Another child of the same parent already holds that `externalId`                        |
| `FAILED_PRECONDITION` (`400`) | The parent is itself a child (`ORGANIZATION_PARENT_IS_CHILD`)                           |
| `FAILED_PRECONDITION` (`400`) | On Stigmer Cloud, the parent's plan does not include child organizations (`child_orgs`) |

<Callout type="warn">
  `parentOrg` and `externalId` cannot change after creation. An update or apply
  keeps the stored values whatever the request carries.
</Callout>

## What your admins can do in a customer's organization

Your Organization's admins are **admins in every child**, with no joining step.
They can do everything a child's own admins can:

- Read and change the child's Agents, Skills, Plugins, shared Vaults, eval runs
  and teams
- Change the child's name, settings, Federation Issuers and sign-in settings
- Grant and remove access: the child's members, roles and invitations
- See the child's billing and its full
  [access history](/docs/guides/authentication/access-history)

They also delete the child, and make someone its owner.

A few things stay private from every admin, yours included, because they belong
to one person rather than to the Organization: a person's conversations
(Sessions), their My vault and their remembered facts (Memories). To read one
person's conversation, mint that person's
[user token](#without-a-federation-issuer-minted-user-tokens) and read it as
them.

A key limited to your Organization, or its service account's key, works in each
child exactly as the account behind it would: an admin service account acts as
an admin in each child, a viewer service account only reads. A key limited to
one child never reaches your Organization or another child.

A member of your Organization who is not an admin has none of these rights over
a child, and the people of one child never see anything another child holds.
Stripe's connected accounts and Twilio's subaccounts work the same way.

`listChildOrgs` pages through all of your Organization's children, newest first
(`pageSize` up to 100; pass the response's `nextPageToken` as `pageToken` to

## Route your customers' sign-ins

Every token a Federation Issuer vouches for works in one Organization only.
Without `externalIdClaim`, that Organization is the Federation Issuer's own
(`acme` here), so your customers' tokens would never reach their child
organizations. Set `externalIdClaim` to the name of the JWT claim that carries
your customer id, and put that claim in every token your auth provider issues
for Stigmer:

```json
{
  "iss": "https://acme.us.auth0.com/",
  "sub": "auth0|507f1f77bcf86cd799439011",
  "aud": "https://api.acme.com/stigmer",
  "org_id": "cust-4411"
}
```

On every request, Stigmer:

1. Reads the claim value from the token (here, claim `org_id` yields
   `cust-4411`)
2. Finds the child of the Federation Issuer's Organization whose `externalId` is
   that value
3. Binds the token to that child: the token works there and nowhere else,
   whatever roles the user holds in other child organizations

Stigmer reads the claim on every token, from a user's first sign-in to their
latest. A user who belongs to several of your customers signs in to each one
with a token that names it.

<Callout type="info">
  **Example.** Alice is a user at two of your customers, Alpha and Beta, and
  holds a role in both child organizations. Her token that claims Alpha is
  refused in Beta, even though she holds a role there. To work in Beta, she
  signs in with a token that claims Beta. If your Federation Issuer sets a
  `signInRole`, the first time she signs in to Beta with a Beta token she
  receives that role in Beta, once.
</Callout>

Set `externalIdClaim` when you register the Federation Issuer in your
Organization:

`externalIdClaim` works on its own: Stigmer binds each token to its child
organization and grants nothing. That suits a platform that creates accounts and
grants roles from its backend, as the next sections show. To have Stigmer create
accounts and grant a role as well, see
[Create accounts and grant a role at sign-in](#create-accounts-and-grant-a-role-at-sign-in).

A Federation Issuer in a child organization cannot set `externalIdClaim`: a
child organization has no children to route to.

### What a bound token can reach

A token bound to a child organization can reach:

- The child organization's resources, as the user's role there allows
- What belongs to no Organization: the user's own Identity Account and API keys
- The Agents, Skills and Plugins your Organization
  [shares with its children](#share-a-catalog-with-every-child), to read and to
  run

It cannot reach anything another parent shares with its children, create an
Organization, or accept an invitation to another Organization.

### Errors

| Error                       | When                                                                                     | What to do                                                                                 |
| --------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `UNAUTHENTICATED` (`401`)   | The token lacks the `externalIdClaim` claim, or its value is not a string                | Put the claim, as a string, in every token your auth provider issues for Stigmer           |
| `UNAUTHENTICATED` (`401`)   | The claim names no child of the Federation Issuer's Organization                         | Create the child organization with that `externalId`, or fix the value your provider sends |
| `PERMISSION_DENIED` (`403`) | The token names one child organization and the request targets another                   | Sign in with a token that names the child organization the request targets                 |
| `PERMISSION_DENIED` (`403`) | The token names the right child organization, but the user's role there doesn't allow it | Grant a role on the child organization                                                     |

Stigmer refuses a missing or unknown claim on every sign-in, returning users
included, and the refusal does not name the value. A first sign-in refused this
way leaves no account behind.

### Without a Federation Issuer: Minted user tokens

A platform whose backend
[mints user tokens](/docs/guides/authentication/product-users) with the API key
of an admin service account of its own Organization routes its customers the
same way: name the child organization as `org` when you mint.

```typescript
declare const user: { id: string; email: string };
// ---cut---
import { createNodeClient } from "@stigmer/client/node";

const stigmer = createNodeClient({
  baseUrl: "https://api.stigmer.ai",
  apiKey: process.env.STIGMER_API_KEY!,
});

const { accessToken } = await stigmer.tokens.mintUserToken({
  org: "tenant-alpha",
  userId: user.id,
  userEmail: user.email,
});
```

`org` may name the service account's own Organization or one of its children: an
admin of the parent is an admin in each of its children. Anything else is
refused with `PERMISSION_DENIED`. The token works in that child only. Accounts
are per child: the same `userId` minted into two children is two accounts, each
created at its first mint into that child, where it receives the sign-in role in
the parent's embedding settings.

## Provision users and place them in children

Account once, under the Organization that owns your Federation Issuer: the `org`
field must be the Federation Issuer reference's own `org`, and Stigmer refuses
any other value with `INVALID_ARGUMENT`. The account belongs to your Federation
Issuer, not to a child. What places the user in a child is the role you grant on
that child organization in the next step, and what lets their token reach it is
the claim that [routes it](#route-your-customers-sign-ins).

## Create accounts and grant a role at sign-in

With `createAccountsOnSignIn` and `signInRole` beside `externalIdClaim`, you can
drop per-user provisioning code. You still create the child organizations, but
Stigmer creates each user's account the first time they sign in and grants the
sign-in role the first time they sign in to each child.

```typescript
const issuer = await stigmer.federationIssuer.create({
  name: "Acme Cloud Auth",
  org: "acme",
  slug: "acme-cloud-auth",
  displayName: "Acme Cloud Auth",
  jwksUri: "https://acme.us.auth0.com/.well-known/jwks.json",
  allowedIssuers: ["https://acme.us.auth0.com/"],
  expectedAudience: "https://api.acme.com/stigmer",
  createAccountsOnSignIn: true,
  signInRole: IamRole.member,
  externalIdClaim: "org_id",
});
```

With this configuration, your platform only needs to:

1. Create each child organization with its `externalId`
2. Put the customer id in a claim in every JWT (for example,
   `"org_id": "cust-4411"`)

The first time a user signs in to a child organization, Stigmer reads the
`org_id` claim, finds your child with that `externalId`, creates the user's
account if they have none, and grants `member` there. When the same user first
signs in to a second child, with a token that names it, Stigmer grants `member`
there too.

<Callout type="info">
  Stigmer grants the sign-in role once per account and child organization. If an
  administrator later removes it, it stays removed, and changing `signInRole`
  does not reach users who already signed in. To give one user a different role,
  grant it from your backend with a Grant. The `owner` role cannot be the
  sign-in role.
</Callout>

## Share a catalog with every child

Set an Agent, Skill or Plugin in your Organization to `visibility_child_orgs`
(the console's **Child organizations** level, or `visibility: child-orgs` in a
skill's frontmatter) and everyone in every child organization can read and run
it, including children you create later. Nobody outside your children can, and
your own Organization keeps seeing it as an Organization-visible resource.
Sessions, runs and Vaults are never shared this way: each child runs the shared
blueprint inside its own boundary.

A child organization can reference your shared blueprint by `org/slug` (an
Agent's skill or MCP server); any other organization's blueprint is refused at
write. A child organization cannot share with children of its own.

## Billing

On Stigmer Cloud, a child organization runs on its parent's plan and has no plan
or invoice of its own; its console billing page reads "Billed to" its parent.
Each child organization beyond those your plan includes appears on your
Organization's invoice at the plan's fee for each; see
[Billing](/docs/concepts/billing#plans).

## Delete a child, or the parent

Your Organization's admins may delete a child organization. An Organization with
a child that is not being deleted cannot be deleted (`FAILED_PRECONDITION`,
`ORGANIZATION_HAS_CHILDREN`): delete its children first. Once every child is
being deleted, the parent can be, and its removal finishes after theirs. A
deleted child's `externalId` is free for a new child once its removal finishes
([Deleting an Organization](/docs/concepts/organizations#deleting-an-organization)).

```typescript
interface PlatformCustomer {
  id: string;
  name: string;
  slug: string;
}
interface PlatformUser {
  oidcSubject: string;
  email: string;
  firstName: string;
  lastName: string;
  avatarUrl: string;
}
declare function provisionUser(user: PlatformUser): Promise<IdentityAccount>;
// ---cut---
async function onboardCustomer(
  customer: PlatformCustomer,
): Promise<Organization> {
  return stigmer.organization.create({
    name: customer.name,
    slug: customer.slug,
    description: `Acme Cloud customer: ${customer.name}`,
    parentOrg: "acme",
    externalId: customer.id,
  });
}

async function onboardCustomerUser(
  customerId: string,
  user: PlatformUser,
): Promise<void> {
  const child = await stigmer.organization.getByExternalId({
    parentOrg: "acme",
    externalId: customerId,
  });

  // One account per user, whichever customers they join.
  const account = await provisionUser(user);

  await stigmer.grant.create({
    principal: { kind: "identity_account", id: account.metadata?.id ?? "" },
    resource: { kind: "organization", id: child.metadata?.id ?? "" },
    relation: "viewer",
  });
}
```

`provisionUser` is the check-then-create handler from
[Provision federated accounts](/docs/guides/authentication/federation/provision-federated-accounts#full-provisioning-handler).
