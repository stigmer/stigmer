Single sign-on (SSO) lets team members in a self-managed Organization sign in to
the Stigmer web console using your Organization's Identity Provider. When a team
member signs in for the first time, Stigmer creates their account automatically.

This is different from
[platform-managed federation](/docs/guides/authentication/federation/overview),
where your backend provisions accounts and your users call the Stigmer API
directly. With SSO, users interact with the Stigmer web console --- not your
app.

The admin sets up SSO once. After that, every team member follows the same path:
visit the SSO login URL, authenticate through your Identity Provider, and land
in the Stigmer console.

## Prerequisites

- A **self-managed Organization** in Stigmer (not platform-managed)
- An **OIDC-compatible Identity Provider** (Auth0, Okta, Entra ID, AWS Cognito,
  or any provider that supports the authorization code flow)
- The Identity Provider must be configured with a **client application** for
  Stigmer (see
  [Configure your Identity Provider](#configure-your-identity-provider) below)

## Configure your Identity Provider

Before enabling SSO in Stigmer, create a client application in your Identity
Provider with these settings:

| Setting                  | Value                                            |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **Application type**     | Single-page application (SPA) or Web application |
| **Allowed callback URL** | `https://app.stigmer.ai/auth/callback`           |
| **Allowed logout URL**   | `https://app.stigmer.ai/login`                   |
| **Grant type**           | Authorization Code (with PKCE for SPAs)          |

<Callout type="tip">
  The callback URL is the same for both SSO and standard authentication. Stigmer
  distinguishes the two flows using browser storage, not separate callback
  endpoints.
</Callout>

After creating the client application, note these values --- you'll need them
when registering the Identity Provider in Stigmer:

- **Client ID** — The OIDC client identifier for the application you just
  created
- **Issuer URL** — Your provider's issuer (the `iss` claim in tokens)
- **JWKS URI** — Your provider's public key endpoint
- **Audience** — The `aud` claim your provider includes in tokens intended for
  Stigmer (not all providers require this --- see the callout below)

<Callout type="info">
  **Audience handling varies by provider.** Auth0 requires an explicit audience
  parameter in the authorization request. Okta and Entra ID include the audience
  automatically based on the client configuration. If your provider doesn't
  require an audience parameter, leave `expectedAudience` empty when registering
  the Identity Provider.
</Callout>

## Register the Identity Provider with SSO enabled

Follow the standard
[Register an Identity Provider](/docs/guides/authentication/federation/register-identity-provider)
guide, but add two SSO-specific fields: `isSsoProvider` and `oidcClientId`.

| Field           | Purpose                                                                                            |
| --------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `isSsoProvider` | Enables SSO automatic provisioning and makes the SSO login URL visible in the console              |
| `oidcClientId`  | The client ID from your Identity Provider. The Stigmer login page uses this to start the OIDC flow |

You can also enable SSO on an existing Identity Provider by editing it in the
Stigmer web console under **Settings > Identity Providers**.

<Callout type="warn">
  Do not enable SSO on platform-managed Organizations. SSO bypasses explicit
  account creation, which conflicts with the platform-managed model where your
  backend controls who gets access.
</Callout>

## Share the SSO login URL

Once SSO is enabled, a login URL appears on the Identity Provider detail panel
in the Stigmer console:

```
https://app.stigmer.ai/login?org=acme
```

The URL includes your Organization slug as the `org` parameter. When a team
member opens this URL, the login page automatically detects the SSO provider and
shows a "Sign in with [provider name]" button.

Copy the URL from the **SSO Login URL** field on the Identity Provider detail
panel and share it with your team through your Organization's internal channels
(email, Slack, intranet, etc.).

<Callout type="tip">
  Team members can also reach the SSO login page by visiting
  `https://app.stigmer.ai/login` and typing your Organization slug manually. The
  URL with `?org=` pre-filled saves them that step.
</Callout>

## What happens on first login

When a team member signs in through SSO for the first time, Stigmer:

1. Validates the JWT from your Identity Provider (signature, issuer, audience,
   expiration)
2. Checks for an existing federated account matching the token's `sub` claim
3. Finds no account, so creates one automatically using profile data from the
   token (`email`, `name`, `picture`)
4. Grants the **viewer** role on the Organization

On subsequent logins, Stigmer skips steps 3 and 4 --- it finds the existing
account and proceeds.

### Why viewer, not member

The viewer role gives read-only access to the Organization's resources. It does
**not** allow creating Agent Executions, which incur usage costs. This protects
the Organization from unexpected charges when new users sign in.

To give a team member full access, an Organization admin must explicitly upgrade
them to the **member** role. See
[Grant access](/docs/guides/authentication/federation/grant-access) for
instructions.

## Manage SSO users

### Upgrade roles

SSO users start as viewers. To upgrade a user to member or admin, use the IAM
Policy API:

```typescript
await stigmer.iamPolicy.create({
  org: "acme",
  resourceType: "organization",
  resourceId: "acme",
  principal: accountId,
  role: "member",
});
```

See [Grant access](/docs/guides/authentication/federation/grant-access) for the
full pattern in all SDK languages.

### Revoke access

To remove an SSO user's access, use `deprovisionFederatedAccount`:

```typescript
await stigmer.identityAccount.deprovisionFederatedAccount({
  org: "acme",
  identityProviderRef: {
    org: "acme",
    kind: "identity_provider",
    slug: "acme-sso",
  },
  externalSub: "auth0|507f1f77bcf86cd799439011",
  deleteAccount: false,
});
```

Set `deleteAccount` to `false` to revoke access while keeping the account (the
user can re-provision via SSO). Set it to `true` for permanent removal.

See
[Revoke a federated account](/docs/guides/authentication/federation/provision-federated-accounts#revoke-a-federated-account)
for the full pattern in all SDK languages.

<Callout type="warn">
  If you revoke-only (without deleting the account) and SSO remains enabled, the
  user can sign in again and regain viewer access through automatic
  provisioning. To permanently block a user, either delete their account or
  disable the Identity Provider.
</Callout>

## Troubleshooting

<Steps>
<Step>

### SSO button doesn't appear on the login page

The login page calls `getSsoProvider` with the Organization slug. If no SSO
provider is found, only the standard sign-in option appears.

**Check**: Confirm that `isSsoProvider` is `true` on your Identity Provider. You
can verify this in the Stigmer console under **Settings > Identity Providers**
or by retrieving the Identity Provider via the SDK and inspecting
`spec.isSsoProvider`.

</Step>
<Step>

### Redirect fails or shows a blank page

The OIDC authorization request depends on the `oidcClientId` and issuer
configured on your Identity Provider.

**Check**:

- Verify `oidcClientId` matches the client application in your Identity Provider
- Verify your Identity Provider's allowed callback URLs include
  `https://app.stigmer.ai/auth/callback`
- Open your provider's OIDC discovery URL
  (`https://{issuer}/.well-known/openid-configuration`) and confirm it's
  accessible from a browser

</Step>
<Step>

### User gets 401 after signing in

Token validation failed. The most common cause is an audience mismatch.

**Check**:

- Decode the JWT (use [JWT.io](https://jwt.io)) and compare the `aud` claim with
  the `expectedAudience` on your Identity Provider
- If your provider doesn't include an `aud` claim by default, configure it to
  add the correct audience or leave `expectedAudience` empty on the Identity
  Provider
- Verify the `iss` claim matches one of the `allowedIssuers`

</Step>
<Step>

### User is provisioned automatically but can't do anything

This is expected. Automatically provisioned users get the viewer role, which is
read-only. An Organization admin must upgrade them to member or admin.

</Step>
</Steps>
