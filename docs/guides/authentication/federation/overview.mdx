Identity federation lets users access Stigmer with tokens issued by an external
authentication system. Your identity provider (Auth0, Okta, …) is registered as
a Federation Issuer, and Stigmer trusts its tokens. Federation is served by
Stigmer Cloud and the Enterprise edition; the open-source self-hosted server
does not serve Federation Issuers.

Federation supports two provisioning modes:

- **JIT provisioning** — Stigmer creates accounts and grants a sign-in role
  automatically when a user authenticates with a valid JWT. Your backend only
  registers the Federation Issuer. Best for platforms where all authenticated
  users should have Stigmer access.
- **Manual provisioning** — Your platform backend creates federated accounts and
  grants roles via the API before users can authenticate. Best for platforms
  that need to control exactly who gets access and when.

Whichever mode you choose, every token a Federation Issuer vouches for is bound
to one Organization and works there only: the Federation Issuer's own
Organization, or the child organization an `externalIdClaim` claim names.

## When you need this

You're building a platform on Stigmer and your users already authenticate
through your own OIDC-compatible provider (Auth0, Okta, Entra ID, AWS Cognito,
or any custom OIDC server). You want those users to interact with Stigmer
Agents, Sessions, and other resources without a separate Stigmer login.

<Callout type="info">
  **Don't have an OIDC provider?** Use a
  [PlatformClient](/docs/guides/authentication/platform-client/overview)
  instead. Your backend mints Stigmer tokens per user — no Federation Issuer
  registration or JWKS endpoint needed.
</Callout>

## What you'll build

By the end of this guide, your platform's users will call the Stigmer API using
JWTs from your auth provider. Stigmer validates each token, resolves the user's
identity, checks access, and processes the request — no separate Stigmer
credentials needed.

## Choose your provisioning mode

|                      | JIT (automatic)                               | Manual (explicit)                                              |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Account creation** | Automatic on first JWT                        | Your backend calls `createFederatedAccount`                    |
| **Role assignment**  | Automatic (`signInRole`)                      | Your backend creates Grants                                    |
| **Setup steps**      | Register a Federation Issuer with JIT enabled | Register a Federation Issuer, provision accounts, grant access |
| **Use when**         | All authenticated users should access Stigmer | You need gated, explicit access control                        |

The setup and runtime differ by provisioning mode. With **manual provisioning**,
your backend performs all four steps above before users authenticate. With **JIT
provisioning**, your backend only registers the Federation Issuer (step

1. — Stigmer handles steps 2 and 3 automatically when a user first
   authenticates. Step 4 is the same regardless of mode: present a JWT, Stigmer
   validates it against your Federation Issuer's JWKS endpoint, resolves the
   caller to a federated account, and checks Grants.

## Prerequisites

Before starting, you need:

- A **Stigmer Organization**
- An **API key** with admin access to that Organization
- An **OIDC-compatible auth provider** with:
  - A JWKS endpoint URL (typically `https://your-domain/.well-known/jwks.json`)
  - The issuer URL (the `iss` claim value in your JWTs)
  - The audience value (the `aud` claim your provider puts in tokens intended
    for Stigmer)

## Your own team's sign-in

A Federation Issuer is trust in tokens your platform sends with its calls to
Stigmer. It is not a way to sign in to the Stigmer web console. To have your
team sign in to a self-hosted Stigmer with your company's identity provider
(Okta, Microsoft Entra ID, Keycloak or any OIDC provider), make it the server's
own sign-in: see
[Enable authentication](/docs/guides/self-hosting/authentication).

## Child organizations

The steps above cover federation for a single Organization. If your platform
serves multiple customers and each customer needs isolated resources, you create
a child organization per customer under your own. With `externalIdClaim`, each
token names its child organization, by your customer id, in a JWT claim and
works in that child only.
