---
title: Keep your customers' keys
description:
  Hold the keys your customers' runs use, one vault per customer, isolated from
  every other customer, and replace a key without touching a conversation.
---

A product built on Stigmer often needs keys that belong to its customers: Acme
Helpdesk's agent reads each customer's Zendesk with that customer's own key.
Stigmer keeps every key in a [vault](/docs/concepts/vaults), and a conversation
uses only the vaults it names. This guide shows how to give each customer a
vault no other customer can name, put a key in it, and replace the key when it
rotates.

## Pick the setup by how your customers sign in

Which setup keeps one customer's keys away from another depends on how your
customers' people reach Stigmer, which you chose on the
[authentication overview](/docs/guides/authentication/overview).

| Your customers' people                                                                                                                  | Where a customer's keys live                                                | Who can name them                                                                             |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Sign in as themselves: the console, or your own sign-in through an [Identity Provider](/docs/guides/authentication/federation/overview) | Each person's own My vault; a team's key in a shared vault the team may use | That person alone; for a shared vault, the people granted its use                             |
| Get a token your backend mints with a [PlatformClient](/docs/guides/authentication/platform-client/overview), all in your Organization  | One private shared vault per customer                                       | On Stigmer Cloud, the customer's accounts you grant **Can use**; on open source, your backend |
| Each customer is a [child organization](/docs/guides/authentication/federation/child-organizations)                                     | A shared vault in that customer's child, with **Organization** visibility   | The child's members, who are that customer's people alone                                     |

<Callout type="warn">
  In one Organization shared by several customers, never give a customer's vault
  **Organization** visibility. Every member and viewer of the Organization may
  then use it, so one customer could name another customer's vault on their own
  conversation.
</Callout>

### People who sign in as themselves

Each person keeps their keys in their own My vault, and your embedded chat
includes it: the React SDK's `NewSessionViewer` includes the sender's My vault
by default for the `"integrator"` and `"endUser"` audiences. Each person's turn
reads their own My vault, never a teammate's. A product that refreshes a
short-lived token for its user saves it into that user's My vault, with that
user's own credential, each time it refreshes. A key a whole team shares goes in
a shared vault the team may use.

### Users your backend signs in with a PlatformClient

The runs of a user signed in through a PlatformClient read no My vault, even
when the conversation includes it, and even a key that user saved in their own
My vault themselves. Give each customer one private shared vault instead. Create
it once, when the customer signs up, with `external_id` set to your own customer
id, and find it again with that id. A run such a user sends checks that whoever
attached the vault to the conversation may still use it.

How the vault reaches the conversation depends on the edition:

- **On Stigmer Cloud**, an admin grants **Can use** on the vault to each of that
  customer's accounts, and your embedded chat names the vault in its `vaults`
  prop. A user who was not granted it cannot name it: the conversation is
  refused.
- **On open source**, roles are granted on the Organization only, so no account
  holds **Can use** on a single vault, and nobody joins a conversation someone
  else created. Your backend creates the conversation with the customer's vault
  and sends the customer's messages itself. To let your users chat directly
  instead, give each customer a child organization (next section).

### A child organization per customer

The customer's vault lives in their child organization, where only that
customer's people are members, so **Organization** visibility is safe there: a
PlatformClient user you mint into the child, and every other member, may use it.
This works in every edition. Your Organization's admins manage a child but read
nothing in it, its vaults included, so the vault is created by an admin of the
child, or by one of your admins after they add themselves to the child, which
the child's member list shows.

## Put a key in, and rotate it

Create the customer's vault with your id for them, then save the key. A value
never rides the command line: `set-secret` reads it from a hidden prompt, a
file, or an environment variable.

```bash
stigmer vault create "cust-4411 keys" --external-id cust-4411
stigmer vault set-secret ZENDESK_API_KEY --vault cust-4411-keys --from-env ZENDESK_API_KEY
```

From your backend, find the vault by your id and save the key into it:

```typescript
const vault = await stigmer.vault.getByExternalId({
  org: "acme-helpdesk",
  externalId: "cust-4411",
});

await stigmer.vault.setSecrets({
  vault: {
    org: "acme-helpdesk",
    vault: { case: "id", value: vault.metadata!.id },
  },
  secrets: { ZENDESK_API_KEY: { value: zendeskKey } },
});
```

Then name the vault on every conversation you start for that customer, in the
Session's `vaults` or the embedded chat's `vaults` prop.

Rotating a key is saving the same name again. Runs keep no copy of a value: each
reads its vaults when its work starts, so the next run uses the new key, and so
does a run you [recover](/docs/concepts/vaults#when-the-values-are-read). No
conversation is sent anything.

## Let a customer sign in to a tool themselves

When the key is the customer's own login to a tool, such as their Slack or their
Linear, send them a Connect link: a one-time page that saves their sign-in into
their vault without a Stigmer account. See
[Connect links for your customers](/docs/concepts/vaults#connect-links-for-your-customers).

## Keys every customer's runs share

A key of your own that every customer's runs use, such as the shared secret of
your own MCP server, goes in one vault named on the PlatformClient, not in each
customer's vault. See
[Give embedded assistants their credentials](/docs/guides/authentication/platform-client/overview#give-embedded-assistants-their-credentials).

## What never goes in a vault

A vault holds keys. A customer's context, such as the project or account they
have open in your product, is not a key, and Stigmer has no per-conversation
place for values. Your own MCP server carries that context: it knows who is
calling from the
[caller identity](/docs/guides/integrations/caller-identity-for-mcp-servers)
Stigmer sends it, and looks up the rest in your product. The
[standing user context](/docs/guides/sharing/embed-an-agent#give-the-agent-standing-user-context)
an embed passes is neither authorization nor a way to send secrets.

## See where a run's values came from

A run's status lists, in `credentials.sources`, every key the run uses with the
vault (by ID) and the entry it came from, never the value. Read it to confirm a
customer's run used that customer's vault.

## Limits

- A conversation names at most 20 vaults.
- One save writes at most 100 secrets.
- An `external_id` is unique in its Organization: two vaults cannot claim the
  same id, even when created at the same moment.
- `getByExternalId` answers `NOT_FOUND` for a vault the caller may not view, the
  same as for one that does not exist.
