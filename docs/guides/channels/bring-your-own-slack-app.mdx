---
title: Bring Your Own Slack App
description:
  Register your Organization's own Slack app so the bot carries your name and
  icon — and so several Agents can serve the same workspace.
---

By default, an Agent connects to Slack through the platform's shared **Stigmer**
app: zero setup, and members chat with a bot named Stigmer. When you want the
bot to carry your own name and icon, register your own Slack app and store it in
Stigmer as a **Channel App**. Each app is its own bot identity, so registering
apps is also how several Agents serve one workspace side by side.

<Callout type="info">
  This guide assumes you have read [Connect an Agent to
  Slack](/docs/guides/channels/connect-slack). A Channel App changes which Slack
  app the connect flow installs — not how the flow itself works.
</Callout>

## When to bring your own app

Two scenarios call for it:

1. **Your brand, not ours.** Members @mention the app's bot name. With the
   platform app that is always `@Stigmer`. With your own app, it is whatever
   name you register — `@Acme Support`, for example.

2. **Several Agents in one workspace.** A workspace hosts one Agent per Slack
   app. To put a second Agent in the same workspace, connect it through a
   different app.

Setup happens in two steps, because each side mints something the other needs:
Slack mints the app's credentials, and Stigmer mints the app's webhook address
once the Channel App exists.

## Step 1 — Create the app and register it

1. In the web console, open **Settings → Channel Apps** and click **Register
   channel app**.
2. Enter the **Name**. This becomes the bot name your workspace will @mention,
   so pick what your team should see — for example `Acme Support Bot`.
3. The form shows a ready-to-paste **Slack app manifest** for that name. On
   [api.slack.com/apps](https://api.slack.com/apps?new_app=1), create a new app,
   choose **From a manifest**, and paste it. The manifest configures the bot
   user, the OAuth redirect URL, and the exact permissions Stigmer needs —
   nothing more.
4. From the new app's **Basic Information** page, copy the **Client ID**,
   **Client secret**, and **Signing secret** into the form. Stigmer encrypts the
   secrets at rest and never shows them again.
5. Click **Register channel app**.

## Step 2 — Finish setup in Slack

The saved Channel App now has its own webhook address — the URL Slack delivers
this app's events to. The app's detail panel shows it under **Finish setup in
Slack**.

1. Copy the **Events request URL** from the detail panel.
2. On your Slack app's **Event Subscriptions** page, enable events and paste it
   as the request URL. Slack verifies the URL immediately — verification passes
   because Stigmer checks the request against the signing secret you registered
   in step 1.

The detail panel also offers the **Completed app manifest** — the same manifest
with the events URL filled in — if you prefer to re-apply it on the app's
manifest page instead.

## Connect an Agent through your app

Registration done, the connect flow picks it up automatically:

1. Open the Agent under **Library → Agents**, go to the **Channels** tab, and
   click **Connect to Slack**.
2. Under **Connect as**, your app now appears next to the platform Stigmer app.
   Select it.
3. Continue the flow as in
   [Connect an Agent to Slack](/docs/guides/channels/connect-slack). Slack's
   consent page shows your app's name, and after the install, members @mention
   your bot — the channel card confirms the serving app.

## What the manifest configures

The generated manifest mirrors the platform app deliberately, with your identity
on it:

- **Four bot scopes** — read mentions, write chat messages, read direct
  messages, and drive the assistant surface. Scope minimalism is deliberate: the
  scope list is the first thing a workspace admin reviews on the consent screen.
- **Seven bot events** — mentions, direct messages, the assistant surface, and
  the uninstall/revocation lifecycle.
- **A shared OAuth redirect URL** — every app, including the platform's,
  completes the install through the console's callback route.
- **No Enterprise Grid** — org-wide installs stay unsupported
  (`org_deploy_enabled: false`), the same as the platform app.

Keep these settings as generated. Trimming scopes or events breaks the channel
in ways that only show up later — a missing event, for example, means messages
silently never arrive.

## Rotate credentials or remove the app

Back in **Settings → Channel Apps**, the app's detail panel manages its
lifecycle:

- **Rotate a secret** — stored secrets display as `***REDACTED***`. Leave a
  field untouched to keep its value, or paste a new one to rotate it — you can
  rotate one secret without re-entering the other.
- **Delete the app** — Stigmer refuses the deletion while any channel still
  installs through the app. Disconnect those channels first, then delete.
