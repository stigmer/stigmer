---
title: Take Over Conversations from Your Agent
description:
  Watch your Agent's customer conversations live, step in and reply through the
  same number, and hand the conversation back when you're done.
---

A connected channel puts your Agent on a real phone number, talking to real
customers. Sooner or later a customer needs a person — they ask for one, or the
question is beyond what the Agent should answer alone. This guide shows you how
to see those conversations, take one over, reply as yourself through the same
number, and hand it back to the Agent when you're done.

<Callout type="info">
  Conversations require Stigmer Cloud and a connected channel. If you haven't
  connected one yet, start with [Connect an Agent to
  WhatsApp](/docs/guides/channels/connect-whatsapp). Staff replies work on
  WhatsApp channels today; Slack conversations are readable, but only the Agent
  replies on them for now.
</Callout>

## Open the Conversations area

**Conversations** is a top-level item in the console sidebar. It lists every
customer conversation across all of your organization's channels, newest
activity first, with the customer's name and number on each row.

Two signals tell you where you're needed:

- The **sidebar badge** counts conversations that want a human right now — the
  Agent escalated, or a customer is waiting on a reply that only a person will
  send. Switch the list from **All** to **Needs human** to see exactly those.
- A **dot on a conversation row** means the customer's last message has not been
  answered yet. A muted dot means the Agent has it — an answer is on its way, no
  action needed. A strong dot means a person holds the conversation, so the
  Agent stays quiet and the reply has to come from your team.

## Read a conversation

Open a row and you get the conversation the way the customer experiences it:
their messages, the Agent's delivered replies, and your teammates' replies, in
order. This is deliberately not the Session viewer — no tool calls, no execution
internals, just what was said on the customer's phone.

Sent messages carry WhatsApp-style ticks. A failed send shows the reason in
plain words right in the bubble — for example, that WhatsApp refused the message
because the customer hasn't written in over 24 hours.

## Take over

Press **Take over** in the conversation header. From that moment the Agent goes
quiet on this one conversation — customer messages still arrive on the timeline,
but the Agent does not answer them. Every other conversation keeps running
normally.

You don't have to press the button first: **sending a reply takes the
conversation over implicitly**. Your message is the takeover.

If the customer writes while your team holds the conversation and nobody has
replied yet, the platform sends them a single acknowledgment — "Someone from our
team is looking at this and will reply here." — so they're never left wondering.
A prompt reply from you replaces it entirely.

## Reply

Type in the composer and send. The reply goes out through the channel's own
number — to the customer it's the same conversation, now with a person in it.
Your message appears on the timeline with delivery ticks once the provider
accepts it.

One WhatsApp rule to know: free-form replies only deliver within 24 hours of the
customer's last message. If the window has closed, the composer warns you before
you send — a reply sent anyway will probably fail, and the window reopens the
moment the customer writes again. Message templates are the lane WhatsApp
provides for reaching out past the window; sending them from the composer isn't
available yet.

## Hand back

Press **Hand back to agent** when you're done. The Agent resumes on the next
customer message — and it resumes informed: it receives a digest of what
happened while it was quiet (the customer's messages and your replies), so it
won't re-answer a question you already settled or greet the customer as if
nothing happened.

If the customer's last message has no reply yet, handing back shows a warning
first. Handing back runs nothing — the Agent stays silent until the customer
writes again — so an unanswered customer would simply wait. Answer first, or
hand back anyway if that's intended.

## When the Agent escalates

Your Agent can decide on its own that a person should look at a conversation — a
customer demanding a human, a request beyond its remit. The conversation is
flagged for attention: it lights up the sidebar badge, joins the **Needs human**
list, and shows a banner with the Agent's reason in the open conversation. The
Agent keeps serving the customer in the meantime rather than leaving dead air.

Two ways to answer the flag: **Take over** (you step in — the flag clears) or
**Dismiss** (a false alarm — the flag clears and the Agent carries on).

## Who can step in

Reading conversations comes with channel view access. Replying, taking over, and
handing back need the **participant** role on the channel. Grant it from the
channel card's menu on the Agent's **Channels** tab — open **Manage access** and
add the teammate. One grant covers every conversation on that channel, not just
one customer.
