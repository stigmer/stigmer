A channel is one way people reach one Agent. Stigmer has three types:

- A **chat link**: a hosted chat page at `app.stigmer.ai/chat/<id>`, and the
  widget your own site embeds.
- **Slack**: the Agent joins a Slack workspace, and people @mention it or
  message it directly.
- **WhatsApp**: the Agent answers a WhatsApp Business phone number.

An Agent can carry several channels at once. The `variance-reporter` Agent might
have a public chat link in your product, a second chat link for one partner, and
a Slack workspace where your own team asks it questions. Each is its own
channel, with its own settings, and each lists on the Agent's **Channels** tab.

If you want to add one now,
[Create a chat link](/docs/guides/channels/create-a-chat-link) and
[Connect an Agent to Slack](/docs/guides/channels/connect-slack) walk through
it. This page explains what a channel is and why it is shaped this way.

## What every channel has

Whatever its type, a channel carries the same few things:

- **Its Agent.** A channel serves exactly one Agent, which lives in the same
  Organization. Deleting the Agent deletes its channels.
- **A switch.** Turning a channel off stops it at once and keeps its settings;
  turning it back on resumes it. Deleting it removes it for good.
- **The vaults its conversations use.** A conversation that arrives through a
  channel has no signed-in person behind it, so its tools use the
  [vaults](/docs/concepts/vaults) the channel names. A chat link for your
  Organization's members names none: members' chats use their own.
- **Run settings.** A channel can pick the model its conversations use and lower
  the Agent's [cost cap](/docs/concepts/billing#set-a-cost-cap) for each
  message. It can only lower a cap, never raise one.

Then each type adds its own settings. A chat link has its audience (your
Organization's members, or anyone with the link), the sites that may embed it
and the messages visitors read at a limit. A Slack or WhatsApp channel has the
workspace or phone number it serves and the app it is installed through.

Every conversation a channel starts is a [Session](/docs/concepts/sessions) of
its Agent, so it reads, scores and costs like any other.

## Who creates a channel

A channel runs the Agent on the Organization's
[provider keys](/docs/guides/integrations/model-provider-keys) for everyone who
reaches it, so creating one is an Organization admin's act. Members who can edit
the Agent but are not admins see no **Add channel** menu.

A channel's conversations have their own roles. A **Participant** reads them and
replies to customers as the business — that is how a person
[takes over a conversation](/docs/guides/channels/take-over-conversations). A
**Viewer** reads them and cannot reply.

## A channel is not sharing

A channel lets people chat with an Agent. It never grants anyone a role on it:
someone chatting through a public chat link cannot see how the Agent is set up,
edit it or read anyone else's conversation. Who may see or change the Agent
stays with **Manage access**, as on every other resource.
[Sharing and access](/docs/concepts/sharing-and-access) explains roles and
general access.

So the words split cleanly. You give an Agent a **chat link** or **connect** it
to Slack; you **share** an Agent, a vault or a conversation when you give people
access to it.

## How it compares

Azure Bot Service and Copilot Studio shape this the same way: one bot or agent,
and many channels, each configured on the bot. Azure's own list of channels
includes Web Chat, Microsoft Teams and Slack, among others
([Configure a bot to run on one or more channels](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/bot-service/bot-service-manage-channels)).

| On Stigmer                                | What you already know                      |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| An Agent with several channels            | An Azure bot connected to several channels |
| A chat link, and the widget a site embeds | Azure's Web Chat channel                   |
| A Slack channel                           | Azure's Slack channel                      |
| The **Channels** tab on the Agent         | The bot's **Channels** settings in Azure   |

## Where Stigmer differs, and why

- **Your own vendor app is a resource of its own.** For Slack, the platform's
  Stigmer app needs no setup, or you can
  [bring your own Slack app](/docs/guides/channels/bring-your-own-slack-app) so
  the bot carries your name and icon. WhatsApp always goes through
  [your Meta app](/docs/guides/channels/set-up-your-meta-app). Either app is a
  **Channel App**, registered once and used by every channel you install through
  it, so its credentials live in one place.
- **A chat link is a channel, not a second feature.** Many platforms put the
  hosted link under "share" or "publish". On Stigmer it lists beside Slack and
  WhatsApp, with the same switch, vaults and run settings, because it is one
  more way people reach the Agent.
- **One Agent, one Organization.** A channel always lives in its Agent's
  Organization: its link, the keys it runs on and its credentials are that
  Organization's. To offer another Organization's Agent, install the Plugin that
  carries it, and give your copy a channel.

## What's next

A channel brings people to an Agent; a conversation is what they have there
([Sessions](/docs/concepts/sessions)). Some jobs need nobody to start them: the
month-end report runs on the first of the month.
[Routines](/docs/concepts/routines) start an Agent on a schedule, from another
system or by hand.
