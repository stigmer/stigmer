---
title: Sharing and access
description:
  Who can see and use what in Stigmer. Learn general access, the Viewer, Editor
  and Owner roles, what an Organization's roles allow, how conversations and
  chat links are shared, and how automation gets an account of its own.
---

Two settings decide who reaches a resource in Stigmer, the same two you know
from Google Drive or GitHub:

- **General access** decides who in your Organization reaches the resource
  without being added to it.
- **A role** decides what one person, or one team, may do with it.

On top of both sits the role each person holds in the Organization itself, which
decides what they may do there at all: an Organization Viewer can be added to an
Agent and still not start a conversation with it.

## General access

Agents, Skills, Plugins and shared vaults have a general access setting. Open
the resource and choose **Manage access** to change it.

| General access          | Who reaches it                                                                                                         |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Private**             | The person who created it, the people it is shared with, and, for an Agent, Skill or Plugin, the Organization's admins |
| **Organization**        | Everyone in the Organization. The default for Agents, Skills and Plugins                                               |
| **Child organizations** | Everyone in every child organization of the Organization. Agents, Skills and Plugins only                              |

An Organization's admins own every Agent, Skill and Plugin in it, whatever its
general access, so a private Agent stays manageable after the person who made it
leaves. A shared vault offers Private and Organization only: its logins and
secrets never leave the Organization. Nothing reaches another Organization
except through
[child organizations](/docs/concepts/organizations#parent-and-child-organizations).

Conversations have no general access setting. A conversation is private to the
person who started it.

## Roles

A role is granted to a person, a team, or a
[service account](/docs/guides/authentication/service-accounts). Stigmer uses
the three roles you know from shared documents:

- **Viewer** sees the resource and changes nothing.
- **Editor** changes it, and never deletes it or decides who has access.
- **Owner** does everything, including deleting it and deciding who has access.

Two kinds of resource use a different word, because Viewer would understate what
the role does there:

- **Participant**, on a conversation or an Agent Channel, reads the conversation
  and sends messages. A Viewer of a conversation only reads it.
- **Can use**, on a shared vault, lets a person's runs use the vault's logins
  and secrets. Handing secrets to a run with a shell is more than reading.

What a role allows is the resource's own: a Viewer of an Agent runs it, a Viewer
of a Skill uses it, a Viewer of a conversation sends nothing. Every kind's roles
and their meaning are listed on
[Roles and general access](/docs/sdk/resources/roles), generated from the API
contract the server enforces.

## Organization roles

Everyone in an Organization holds one of four roles in it:

| Role       | What it allows                                                                                                                                             |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Owner**  | Everything, including deleting the Organization and choosing its owners                                                                                    |
| **Admin**  | Manage people, settings, billing, and the Organization's Agents and tools; cannot read private conversations, delete the Organization or change its owners |
| **Member** | Start conversations with the Agents shared with them, and create Agents when the Organization allows it                                                    |
| **Viewer** | See what is shared with the Organization; cannot start conversations                                                                                       |

**Members can create Agents** while the Organization's **Members can create
agents** policy is on, which it is by default; an admin turns it off under
**Settings > Policies**, and then only admins create Agents. A member's Agent is
theirs: private or shared with the Organization, never with child organizations,
which stays an admin's choice. Skills and Plugins are installed for the whole
Organization by its admins.

The two kinds of role combine. Starting a conversation with an Agent needs both
a role on the Agent (Viewer or higher, or general access that reaches you) and
the Organization's Member role or higher. An Organization Viewer sees the Agents
shared with the Organization and how they are set up, and starts no conversation
with them.

## Conversations

A conversation is private to the person who started it. Its Organization's
admins do not read it. In the Enterprise and Cloud editions, its owner may share
it with people, one by one:

- A **Participant** reads the conversation and sends messages. Each message runs
  in the conversation's own workspace, as the person who started it, with the
  vaults the conversation names. Stopping a run and approving a tool call stay
  with the conversation's owner.
- A **Viewer** reads the conversation and sends nothing.

A conversation is never shared with a team. Sending a message also needs
permission to run the conversation's Agent, asked again on every message:
[Who may send a message](/docs/concepts/sessions#who-may-send-a-message).

## Chat links

A chat link is a hosted chat page for one Agent. It reaches signed-in members of
your Organization unless someone picks **Anyone with the link**, and only that
explicit choice puts it on the internet. A chat link spends the Organization's
credits with everyone who holds it, so creating one, and connecting an Agent to
a channel such as Slack, is an admin's act.

A chat link lets people chat with the Agent. It does not let them see how the
Agent is set up or change it: that stays with the Agent's general access and
roles. [Share an Agent](/docs/guides/sharing/share-an-agent) walks through it.

## Automation

A CI job, a script or a backend that calls Stigmer should not use a person's API
key: it stops working when that person leaves, and everything it does is
recorded as them. Give it a
[service account](/docs/guides/authentication/service-accounts), your
Organization's own account for automation. A service account holds one
Organization role, never Owner, acts only through API keys your admins create
for it, and works only in its own Organization.

## Editions

Every edition has general access, the Organization's four roles, chat links and
service accounts. The open-source edition grants roles on the Organization only.
Granting a role on one resource, to a person, a team or a service account, and
so sharing a conversation, is a feature of the Enterprise and Cloud editions.
Teams are Enterprise and Cloud too.
