A routine is a saved Agent invocation: which Agent to run, what to tell it, and
the workspace and vaults its runs use. It starts in three ways:

- **On its schedule.** A routine with a schedule runs on its own, for example
  every weekday at nine in the morning.
- **When someone runs it.** **Run now** on the routine's page, or
  `stigmer routine fire`, starts it at once.
- **When another system calls it.** Your alerting tool, a deploy pipeline or a
  script starts it with one HTTP request, and can pass a note describing what
  happened.

Two examples show the range. A workshop's support Agent sends fee reminders
every morning: a routine with a schedule and a message, "Send fee reminders to
members whose dues fall in the next 3 days", that nobody touches after it is
made. An engineering team's triage Agent has no schedule at all: their alerting
tool starts it whenever an alert fires, and passes the alert's text as the note,
so the Agent investigates that alert.

If you want to make one now,
[Run an Agent on a schedule](/docs/guides/routines/run-an-agent-on-a-schedule)
and
[Start a routine from another system](/docs/guides/routines/start-a-routine-from-another-system)
walk through it. This page explains what a routine is and why it is shaped this
way.

## What every routine has

- **Its Agent and message.** A routine runs exactly one Agent, in the same
  Organization, and every run starts from the same message. Deleting the Agent
  deletes its routines.
- **An optional schedule.** A cron expression, such as `0 9 * * 1-5`, kept in
  the routine's time zone. Without one, the routine runs only when someone or
  something starts it.
- **A switch.** Turning a routine off stops it from running on its schedule and
  refuses every start, and keeps its settings and history. Turning it back on
  resumes it. Deleting it removes it for good and leaves its past runs alone.
- **The vaults its runs use.** No one is signed in when a routine runs, so its
  tools use the [vaults](/docs/concepts/vaults) the routine names.
- **Its history.** Every start is recorded: when it happened, whether the
  schedule or a person or system started it, who that was, and the run it began
  or the reason it did not begin.

Each run is a new [Session](/docs/concepts/sessions) of its Agent, told the date
and time in the routine's time zone, so it reads, scores and costs like any
other conversation.

## A note from whoever starts it

A start by hand or from another system can carry a note of up to 65,536
characters: an alert body, a failing log, a customer's ticket. The Agent
receives it after the routine's message, inside a block marked as information
from whoever started the run, not instructions. So the routine's own message
decides what the Agent does with it, as in "Investigate the alert in the note".
A note can tell the Agent what happened; it cannot change what the routine asks
the Agent to do. That matters because a note can come from a script holding a
key, not from the person who wrote the routine.

## Who a routine acts as

Every run acts as the routine's owner: the person or service account that
created it. It runs with the owner's access, uses the owner's place in the
Organization, and its conversation is the owner's. That holds whoever started
the run: a teammate pressing **Run now** or an alerting tool calling it does not
change who the run acts as.

A routine's owner is only the account that created it. No one else can be made
its owner, and an Organization's admins do not become its owner by being admins.
Only the owner changes what a routine runs: its message, its schedule, its
workspace, its vaults. Anyone else who manages it can switch it off, but not
change the words and credentials that run in the owner's name.

A routine that must keep running when a person leaves is created by a
[service account](/docs/guides/authentication/service-accounts), so it acts as
that account rather than as a teammate.

## Who may start it and read its conversations

The owner always may. Others need a role on the routine, which only the owner
grants, to people, Teams or service accounts:

- A **Participant** starts it, with or without a note, and reads its
  conversations. A service account that is a Participant is how another system
  starts a routine.
- A **Viewer** reads the routine, its history and its conversations, and starts
  nothing.

An Organization's admins see every routine, its history and who has access, and
may switch it off, rename, relabel, resume or delete it, and remove someone's
access. They do not start it or read its conversations: the run speaks with the
owner's authority, and its conversation is the owner's.

Granting a role on one routine is part of the Enterprise and Cloud editions. On
open source, only a routine's owner starts it, by hand or with the owner's own
key; a routine meant for automation there is created with a service account's
key, and that key starts it. On a laptop, routines run while `stigmer up` is
running.

By default a routine starts by hand at most 30 times an hour, counting **Run
now** and calls from other systems together; past that, a start is refused until
the hour moves on. Starts on its schedule are not counted.

## Off and paused

A routine can stop running on its schedule for two reasons, with two remedies:

- **Off** (disabled) is its owner's choice. The owner turns it back on.
- **Paused** is the platform's: after repeated failed runs on its schedule, it
  stops the routine and records why. Fix the cause, then resume it from the
  routine's page or with `stigmer routine resume`.

A routine that is off refuses every start. A paused routine can still be started
by hand, which is how you test a fix before resuming it.

## How it compares

Claude Code's routines
([Automate work with routines](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/routines)) are
the closest idea, and Stigmer uses their words: a saved configuration that runs
on a schedule, through **Run now**, or from an API call with a `text` note.

| On Stigmer                                          | What you already know from Claude Code                 |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| A routine: an Agent, a message, a workspace, vaults | A routine: a prompt, repositories, connectors          |
| Its schedule, **Run now**, another system's call    | Its schedule, API and GitHub triggers, and **Run now** |
| The note, marked as information, not instructions   | The `text`, marked as data, not instructions           |
| Every run acts as the routine's owner               | Runs act as you                                        |
| At most 30 starts by hand an hour                   | 30 Run now and API fires an hour, per routine          |

Claude Managed Agents' scheduled deployments
([Scheduled deployments](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/managed-agents/scheduled-deployments))
share two more choices: a manual run is allowed while the deployment is paused,
and every attempt is recorded with whether the schedule or a manual call started
it.

## Where Stigmer differs, and why

- **A service account holds the key, not the routine.** Claude Code gives each
  routine its own bearer token. Stigmer has one access model: a service account
  with a key, granted Participant on the routine, so a key is revoked, rotated
  and audited like any other. The key also carries its account's Organization
  role, at least Viewer, so it reaches what that role reaches; the routine's
  **API** tab says so before you create one.
- **Two switches, two writers.** The owner's on and off switch lives in the
  routine's settings, and the platform's pause after repeated failures lives
  beside it in what the platform observes, so applying a manifest never clears a
  pause and a pause never looks like the owner's choice.
- **No minimum interval.** Claude Code refuses a schedule that runs more often
  than hourly. A Stigmer routine may run every minute; the hourly limit applies
  to starts by hand, and a schedule never starts a run while its last one is
  still going.
