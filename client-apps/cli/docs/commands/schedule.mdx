Operate on schedules: fire one immediately, or clear a platform pause.

A schedule runs an agent on a cron, and it can stop firing for two different
reasons with two different remedies. **Disabled** is the owner's switch
(`spec.enabled: false` in the manifest) — re-apply with `enabled: true` to
turn it back on. **Paused** is the platform's failure latch: after repeated
failed runs the platform pauses the schedule and records the reason on
status. Applying the manifest never clears a pause; `stigmer schedule
resume` is the one path that does.

The declarative lifecycle rides the unified verbs: `stigmer apply -f` to
create or update, `stigmer get schedule` to inspect (including the pause
reason and failure streak), `stigmer list schedule` to see every schedule's
enabled/paused state at a glance, and `stigmer delete schedule` to tear one
down along with its firing clock.

### Common patterns

```bash
# Fire a schedule once, right now — the fastest way to validate one
stigmer schedule trigger my-org/daily-fee-reminders

# See why a schedule stopped firing
stigmer get schedule my-org/daily-fee-reminders

# Clear a platform pause after fixing the underlying failure
stigmer schedule resume my-org/daily-fee-reminders

# Survey every schedule in the org
stigmer list schedule --org my-org
```

### Disabled versus paused

| State | Set by | Visible in | Cleared by |
|-------|--------|------------|------------|
| Disabled | The owner (`spec.enabled: false`) | `ENABLED` column of `stigmer list schedule` | Re-apply the manifest with `enabled: true` |
| Paused | The platform, after repeated failed runs | `STATE` column, with the reason in `stigmer get schedule` | `stigmer schedule resume` |

A schedule can be enabled by its owner yet paused by the platform at the
same time — that combination means the schedule is broken, not switched off,
and resuming it without fixing the failure only restarts the streak.

### Trigger semantics

`stigmer schedule trigger` fires through the same path as a scheduled tick:
a disabled or paused schedule refuses to fire, the run counts toward the
failure streak, and a successful run resets it. Use it to validate a new
schedule immediately instead of waiting for the next cron boundary.
